Buying the gear was not the end of it.
In China, a vendor that sold AI cheating devices sent threatening messages to buyers, demanding 300,000 yuan — about 60 million won — in exchange for keeping their purchases secret.
Some buyers reportedly paid.
The moment someone buys cheating gear, the purchase record becomes another weakness.

> **In one line, a Chinese AI cheating-gear vendor is accused of demanding 300,000 yuan to keep buyers' purchase records secret, the extortion works because cheating itself is illegal and victims avoid reporting it, and the same gear became a problem in Korea with the tax accountant top scorer's confession and the November 19 college entrance exam.**

New models keep getting better at exactly the work exams measure — the [GPT-6.2 Astra launch](https://aitechnotesen.blogspot.com/2026/11/gpt-62-astra-is-herecheaper-and-faster.html) scored 62 on the Intelligence Index, and Astra answers science questions at the top of its class. When AI solves exam questions at that level, the exam itself has to change.

## Why the Threats Worked

![The back of a person covering their face while looking at a phone screen in a dark room](images/02-img1-en.jpg)

The back of a person covering their face while looking at a phone screen in a dark room

The threat is simple.
Pay, or the purchase record goes public.
One buyer who received the message said it was genuinely frightening.
A public purchase record would expose the cheating itself.

Experts say the reason the threats work is clear (expert view).
Cheating itself is illegal, so victims avoid reporting it.
A structure where people cannot report because their own illegal act would surface is what makes the extortion possible.

The vendor reportedly sold AI glasses and tiny cameras for cheating.
It held buyer lists and purchase records and used them to threaten buyers.
The seller knew exactly where its buyers were vulnerable.

Chinese social media criticized the case as cheating gear turning into a tool for a new kind of crime.
It goes beyond exam cheating into threats and extortion, a second crime.
The reality that purchase records become tools for threats shows a structural problem in this market, critics say.

## How the Gear Market Grew

![A laptop screen showing an online marketplace listing small electronic gadgets](images/03-img2-en.jpg)

A laptop screen showing an online marketplace listing small electronic gadgets

Behind the controversy sits explosive growth in the AI cheating-gear market.
Chinese online marketplaces still sell AI glasses and other cheating devices, according to reports.
Chinese authorities are cracking down, but the gear is so small and discreet that catching it is hard.

The gear keeps getting smaller.
AI glasses carry a camera and a speaker, letting wearers ask AI what they see and hear the answer.
They look like ordinary glasses, so telling them apart is hard.
CNN reported that Meta and Ray-Ban AI glasses alone sold more than 7 million units last year.

The tiny camera the tax accountant top scorer reportedly used is 2 millimeters across.
About as thick as a pencil lead, it barely shows when attached to clothing.
A camera checked by a broadcaster's reporting team read text clearly even in the dark and scored full marks.
Confiscating phones does not help.
The device that produces the answers can sit outside the exam hall as long as the camera is on the body.

How easy the method has become is also on record.
One case built an app that photographs problems and solves them with Claude in 10 minutes.
A camera hidden on a wrist showed new questions from a tax accountant exam, and solving them took 5.3 seconds, with all three answers correct.
Anyone who can handle a little programming can get such a tool with no accomplice.

Interest is surging too.
Searches for AI glasses on online marketplaces rose 185% in a week.
Exam forums carried posts saying AI glasses and tiny cameras were sold out.
Imports of tiny specialty cameras through July this year were reported at nearly 20 times the level of four years ago.
Sellers are even using the scandal in marketing.
An overseas vendor posted in a messenger channel that it had helped the tax accountant top scorer, and Korean exam takers flooded it with inquiries afterward.

## How Korea Tried to Stop It

![A bright exam hall with rows of desks and an invigilator walking between them](images/04-img3-en.jpg)

A bright exam hall with rows of desks and an invigilator walking between them

The same gear is already a problem in Korea.
AI glasses cheating was caught more than five times this year in exams run by the Human Resources Development Service of Korea, and also appeared in TOEIC and middle and high school exams.
In Gwangju in May, an invigilator spotted AI glasses from a student's odd eye movements.
In a gas technician exam in October, dedicated staff found one person using an app that catches Bluetooth signals and a metal detector.

The biggest shock was the tax accountant top scorer's confession.
The top scorer, who earned 397.5 out of 400 on the first stage, appeared in person on November 15, admitted the cheating, and turned himself in to police the same day.
He said he photographed questions with a tiny camera, sent them to generative AI, and checked the answers on a smartphone.
The National Tax Service has started proceedings and plans to void the exam result and suspend exam eligibility for five years after a hearing.

The November 19 college entrance exam response had two lines.
One stops cheating on site, the other traces it afterward.
The education ministry added smart glasses, smart rings, and camera pens to the banned-items list.
Before the fourth session, glasses were placed on desks for inspection, and belongings were rechecked before the third session.
About 1,800 staff for smart-device checks and 6,600 hallway invigilators were added, roughly 8,400 people in total.
Metal detectors were used on suspicion.
Detectors that catch radio signals were not ready this year and will be considered from next year's exam.

Tracing continues after the exam ends.
Scores are analyzed, and if suspicion arises, AI service access records and communication records can be requested.
The government says global AI companies and telecom carriers will cooperate with post-exam investigations from the 2027 college entrance exam.
The aim is to trace cheating that was not caught on site by matching AI service use records and communication logs from exam hours.

Punishment is getting heavier.
Possessing a banned device alone voids the exam, and using AI glasses blocks the next year's exam.
The government is pushing to extend the exam ban period.
The education minister mentioned three years and the prime minister up to ten years; neither is settled yet.
The current college entrance exam ban is one year.

China chose a different path.
During the gaokao, the national college entrance exam, major AI apps switch off the feature that reads photos and solves problems.
Companies such as DeepSeek, Tencent, and Alibaba suspended image recognition during exam hours.
Critics still call it a rushed measure made two days before the exam, one that ultimately leans on invigilators' eyes.
A tiny camera hidden in clothing is especially hard to catch with it.

For anyone facing an exam, removing the option of buying the gear comes first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I do if I receive a threatening message?**

Extortion is a separate crime of its own. The threat counts on the fear that the purchase record will expose the cheating, so paying makes repeat threats more likely. Reporting to police is the right move.

**Is buying AI glasses itself illegal?**

AI glasses are ordinary electronics on their own. The problem is using them in an exam. Using AI glasses in an exam voids that exam and blocks the next year's college entrance exam. Tiny specialty cameras with radio certification reportedly cannot be banned from sale under current law.

**What happens if AI cheating is caught in the college entrance exam?**

Possessing a banned device alone voids the exam. Using AI glasses blocks the next year's exam. After the exam, score analysis can lead to requests for AI service access records and communication records. The exam ban is currently one year, with proposals to extend it to three or up to ten years under discussion.
