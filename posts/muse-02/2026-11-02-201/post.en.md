A Webcam That Follows You — Insta360 Link 2 vs OBSBOT Tiny 2, What to Check Before Buying

A fixed webcam frustrates anyone who does not sit still. When you present standing up or walk around the room showing something, the camera has to follow you. The Insta360 Link 2, the OBSBOT Tiny 2, and the lighter Tiny 2 Lite are all gimbal 4K webcams. Their sensors, prices, and software conditions differ, so the things to check before buying differ too. This is not a hands-on review; it is a pre-purchase check based on official materials and independent reviews.

## What These Products Are

According to official specifications (checked 2026-11-02), the Link 2 uses a 1/2-inch CMOS sensor, the Tiny 2 uses a larger 1/1.5-inch CMOS sensor, and both support 4K at 30 fps and 1080p at 60 fps. According to the manufacturer, the Tiny 2's larger sensor aims for a wider dynamic range and more natural color. The Lite uses the same 1/2-inch CMOS sensor as the Link 2.

## Three Interesting Features

First, tracking. The Link 2 offers a tracking fence that sets tracking boundaries, a whiteboard mode that straightens the board image, and a desk view from above. The Tiny 2 auto-adjusts zoom while tracking and supports hand tracking and zone tracking.

Second, privacy. The Link 2 tilts its lens down after 10 seconds of inactivity, and OBSBOT models hide the feed with a sleep mode.

Third, software. Advanced features are set in the Link 2's Link Controller and the Tiny 2 family's OBSBOT Center. A basic webcam works by just plugging it in, but tracking, gestures, and background effects effectively require installing the software. System requirements are Windows 10 and macOS 10.13 or later for the Link 2, and Windows 10 and macOS 11 or later for the Tiny 2 family. Whether an account is required is unconfirmed.

## Drawbacks and Things to Check

Independent reviews commonly point out that AI features are not always consistent. A Link 2 review reported that tracking and auto white balance and exposure are intermittently unstable, and a Tiny 2 Lite review reported that gesture recognition is slow and intermittently misses. Most meeting apps support only up to 1080p, so 4K often remains for recording and streaming. Check the connection too. The Link 2 uses USB-C 2.0, the Tiny 2 uses USB 3.0, and the Lite's USB version could not be confirmed in the official spec excerpt. The Tiny 2's 4K beauty and background effects require Windows RTX 2060 or higher, or Mac M1 or higher.

## Price and Availability in Korea

The Link 2 is $199.99 at a US retailer, the Tiny 2 is $299 at the official store, and the Lite is $159 at the official store. Prices vary by region and promotion, so check the final price before paying. In Korea, Tiny 2 Lite sales on Coupang are confirmed, with a lowest price of 269,000 KRW according to a news article. The Link 2's official price in Korea and Tiny 2 (non-Lite) sales in Korea are unconfirmed, and an overseas-purchase agency listed the Link 2 at 527,290 KRW including duties and taxes. After-sales service conditions in Korea are also unconfirmed. The Link 2 Pro and Link 2C Pro announced in 2026 are separate new models.

## Verdict

If you often use a whiteboard, the Link 2 comes first; if you broadcast while moving around, the Tiny 2's auto zoom and larger sensor come first; if you want gimbal tracking on a lower budget, the Tiny 2 Lite comes first. If you only meet seated and never record in 4K, all three may be more than you need. The final pre-purchase list is meeting-app resolution, computer specifications, USB ports and lighting, and sales and after-sales conditions in Korea.
