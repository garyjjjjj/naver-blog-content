Nothing Ear (3) | The Case Is the Microphone, Worth $179?

If you take a lot of calls in noisy places, it deserves a look. If battery life and iPhone integration matter more, the AirPods Pro 3 is the better pick. ## What Is the Nothing Ear (3)?

Nothing, a British brand, released its third flagship wireless earbuds on September 18, 2025. They follow the Nothing Ear from April 2024. The look is a transparent stem with metal accents. **The case is made from 100% recycled aluminium, finished through 27 processes.** Each earbud weighs 5.2 g, and the whole set with the case is 71.4 g. They come in black and white.

## How Does a Case Work as a Microphone?

There is a round TALK button on the front of the case. **Hold the button and keep the case within 5 cm of your mouth, and the dual microphones inside pick out only your voice.** According to Nothing, it isolates your voice even with ambient noise up to 95 dB, about the level inside a subway car. Each earbud also carries three directional microphones and a bone-conduction sensor that reads vibrations from the jaw and ear canal to find your voice. Nothing says wind noise drops by more than 25 dB.

A TechRadar reviewer tried the Super Mic on a windy coast and wrote that the person on the other end called it "much better". The same review noted that holding a case to your mouth can look odd, a little like talking into a walkie-talkie.

The Super Mic does more than calls. **Nothing Phone owners can press the TALK button to record a voice note straight into Essential Space.** The recording is transcribed automatically. Automatic transcription needs Nothing OS, and it is unconfirmed on iPhone. Calls themselves work on iPhone too.

## How Does It Sound?

The 12 mm driver leans into bass. TechRadar called the sound "exciting and full-bodied" and gave sound quality 4 out of 5. **The default tuning is bass-heavy, so adjusting the 8-band EQ in the Nothing X app is worth doing.** LDAC supports high-resolution streaming, but iPhones do not support LDAC. Spatial audio is static only and does not track head movement.

Noise cancelling reaches up to 45 dB. Nothing says the profile is recalculated every 600 ms, and ear-tip leakage is checked every 1,875 ms. **TechRadar wrote that the noise cancelling did not feel better than on the older Ear (a).** The 45 dB figure is the same as the older model. Low subway rumble is handled well, but higher sounds such as voices are not fully erased.

## Where Does It Fall Short?

Battery life comes first. **With ANC on, the earbuds alone last 5.5 hours.** The AirPods Pro 3 manage 8 hours with ANC on. A two-hour commute each way means putting them back in the case every other day. With the case the total is 22 hours, enough for a week of use. A 10-minute charge gives 10 hours of playback, which softens the blow.

Price is second. **The older Nothing Ear (a) now sells for $89.** That is half the price of the Ear (3). The noise-cancelling figure is the same, and reviews describe a similar sound. The question is whether the Super Mic, spatial audio, and metal case justify $90 more. How-To Geek gave it 6 out of 10, noting muffled earbud microphones and ANC that changes the sound character.

Third is the fit with iPhone. LDAC only shines on Android, and Essential Space transcription needs a Nothing phone. **iPhone owners should count on using only about half of what makes the Ear (3) different.** For them, the AirPods Pro 3 heart-rate sensor, live translation, and automatic device switching may matter more.

## Price and Buying in Korea

The US price is $179. **At 1,344.44 won per dollar (November 25, 2026), that converts to about 241,000 won.** The Korean list price is 249,000 won, and the preorder price was 219,000 won. Coupang listed it at 208,050 won as of November 17. Once shipping and taxes are added to the US price, the gap to the Korean list price is small.

Against the AirPods Pro 3, the difference is clear. **At Korean list prices, it is 120,000 won cheaper than the AirPods Pro 3 at 369,000 won.** In US prices the gap is $70. The AirPods Pro 3 have been seen at 296,000 won on Coupang, so the real street-price gap can be smaller. Check both prices on the day you buy.

It launched officially in Korea, so it is available through official channels. The local after-sales procedure is unconfirmed.

## Check Before You Buy

- ✅ You take calls and voice notes in noisy places often
- ✅ You use an Android phone and listen to LDAC audio
- ✅ You like the transparent design and metal case
- ⚠️ 5.5 hours with ANC on feels short for you
- ⚠️ You only use an iPhone, so the extras shrink
- ⚠️ Compare it with the half-price Ear (a) first

## My Take

The Nothing Ear (3) is mostly a calling earbud. **If holding the case to your mouth does not bother you, $179 is the price of clear calls in noisy places.** On sound and noise cancelling alone, there is little daylight from the half-price older model. So the call is simple. If call quality comes first and you use Android, it is worth buying. If battery life and iPhone integration come first, the AirPods Pro 3 is the better pick.

For frequent callers on Android, the Super Mic alone can justify $179.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the Super Mic work on iPhone?**

Calls work. Pressing the TALK button to call through the case microphone works on any phone. Automatic voice-note transcription and Essential Space saving need a Nothing phone.

**Does it stop working if I do not hold the case to my mouth?**

Nothing recommends staying within 5 cm. The farther the case, the more ambient noise mixes in. The earbuds' own microphones still take calls, but the Super Mic advantage shrinks.

**Does it support wireless charging?**

Yes. It supports USB-C wired charging and wireless charging. A full wired charge takes 70 minutes, a full wireless charge 120 minutes.
