A Mouse Whose Ball Moves Under Your Thumb — Keychron T1 HE, What to Check Before Buying

A narrow desk makes every mouse movement a chore. The free space beside the laptop is barely palm-sized. A trackball mouse leaves its body still and moves the cursor by rolling a ball with the thumb. The T1 HE goes one step further. It is a trackball mouse whose ball position itself can be moved to fit your thumb. Global pre-orders began in November 2026 (Keychron official product page, checked 2026-11-15).

## What is this mouse?

The Keychron T1 HE is a wireless trackball mouse from Keychron, a keyboard company. Following the bar-shaped Nape Pro, it is the company's second trackball, with a 34 mm trackball in a right-handed body shaped like a regular mouse (Notebookcheck, 2026-11-12). The ball sits on the left side of the body, where the thumb rests. You only roll your thumb, so it suits a narrow desk. It measures 119.4 × 81.3 × 53.3 mm and weighs 160 ± 3 g (official product page, checked 2026-11-15).

## Move the ball to your thumb

The long-standing complaint about trackballs is that the ball never fits your hand. Hand sizes and thumb lengths differ, yet the ball stayed in one fixed spot. The T1 HE moves the whole ball housing with two dials on the underside. It adjusts 3–3.5 mm left-right and front-back, and 2–2.5 mm up-down (official product page, checked 2026-11-15). A few millimeters sounds small, but it changes the angle at which your thumb meets the ball.

The ball comes out for cleaning. Push it out through the hole on the underside with the included screwdriver and wipe it with a dry cloth (official product page FAQ, checked 2026-11-15). Official replacement balls are planned, but whether third-party 34 mm balls fit is unconfirmed. Check compatibility before swapping to a ball of a different weight.

## Adjust the click feel too

The left and right buttons use Keychron's MagOptic switches. You can switch between optical and magnetic modes, and in magnetic mode you can set the actuation point — the depth at which a click registers — across 12 levels (per the official product page, checked 2026-11-15). A click can register with a shallow press or only after a deeper one. Rapid Trigger is supported for fast repeated clicks. The claimed switch lifespan of up to 100 million clicks is the manufacturer's claim.

Cursor speed is adjusted separately. It uses a PixArt 3212 sensor, and the button on the body cycles through five presets from 100 to 1,200 DPI. Keychron Launcher, the web-based configuration tool, raises it to a maximum of 2,400 DPI. All six buttons can be remapped in Launcher, and settings are stored in the mouse's onboard memory. Settings can only be changed over a wired or 2.4 GHz connection, not over Bluetooth (official pre-launch page, checked 2026-11-15).

## Drawbacks and things to check

First, the hand. It is right-handed only, so left-handed users cannot use it. Moving the ball does not make it left-handed.

Second, the official pages disagree on specifications. The pre-launch page lists 11 magnetic-mode actuation levels and up to 116 hours of Bluetooth battery life, while the product page lists 12 levels and 106 hours (at 125 Hz). There is no official explanation of which is final. Check the product page again before pre-ordering.

Third, nobody has used one for long yet. It is in global pre-order, so there are no long-term hands-on reviews. Whether adjusting the ball position actually reduces fatigue, or whether a few millimeters is noticeable, is unverified. If this is your first trackball, allow time to adapt.

Fourth, the condition for changing settings. To change buttons, macros, or actuation points, you must connect by cable or 2.4 GHz. If you switch among three Bluetooth devices, keep the cable or receiver handy for setup.

## My verdict

The Keychron T1 HE suits people who use a mouse for long hours on a narrow desk with barely any wrist movement. The idea of fitting the ball position to your hand is itself the reason to buy it. Adjustable click feel and three connection modes are a bonus. It is not for left-handed users, people who need a mouse right away rather than a pre-order, or anyone unsure whether a trackball will suit them. If you buy one, check that it is right-handed only, re-check the final specifications on the product page, and calculate the real price including shipping to Korea and customs before pre-ordering.
