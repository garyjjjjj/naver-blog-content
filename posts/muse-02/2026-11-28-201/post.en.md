DJI Mic 3 | A Wireless Mic Without a Lav Input, Worth $259?

Anyone who records voices on camera has thought about a wireless mic at some point. The DJI Mic 3 is a wireless mic with a 16-gram transmitter that sits on a shirt without pulling it down, and 32-bit float recording that keeps loud sounds from clipping. It costs $259 in the US and 393,000 won in Korea. This post pulls together the official material and independent reviews.

## What Is the DJI Mic 3?

DJI released it on August 28, 2025. The standard kit holds two transmitters, one receiver, and a charging case. **Each transmitter weighs 16 g with the magnet, and 12.5 g for the body alone.** That is 12 g lighter than the 28-gram Mic 2 transmitter. The receiver carries a 1.1-inch AMOLED touchscreen and a dial. You clip or magnet-mount the transmitter on clothing and connect the receiver to a camera or a phone. DJI cameras pair with the transmitter directly over Bluetooth, so no receiver is needed.

## What Changes When a 16-Gram Transmitter Clips On?

It does not drag down a thin shirt, and it stays less visible on screen. **At 16 g, it weighs a little more than two 500-won coins.** It attaches with a magnet or a clip. The windscreen stays on while the transmitter sits in the charging case. The case charges both transmitters and the receiver at once. TechRadar praised it as roughly half the size with better performance.

## Recording That Does Not Clip When Sound Gets Loud

Each transmitter keeps its own copy of the recording. **Dual-file 32-bit float recording keeps the waveform intact when sound suddenly gets loud.** Built-in memory holds 32 GB per transmitter, about 43 hours in 32-bit float. A burst of laughter or applause can still be saved in the edit.

But 32-bit float is not the default. You have to turn it on in settings. **Only the processed file is 32-bit float, while the unprocessed safety track is a 24-bit file.** Adaptive gain control raises quiet voices and lowers loud ones. Three voice tone presets change the character of the voice. Noise cancelling has two levels. The basic level trims background sound, while the strong level removes nearly all of it but leaves the voice sounding processed, a point several reviews repeat.

## Timecode and Four Transmitters: Less Time in the Edit

Matching sound to picture across several cameras eats editing time. **Timecode gives the transmitters and the receiver the same clock, so they sync without waveform matching.** DJI states drift of less than one frame in 24 hours. One receiver links up to four transmitters and outputs four channels at once. A four-person panel records in one take.

**DJI cameras connect with the transmitter alone, no receiver needed.** That covers the Osmo Action 6 and 5 Pro, Action 4, Osmo Nano, Osmo 360, Osmo Mobile 8 and 7P, and Osmo Pocket 4 and 4P and 3. A single transmitter costs 120,000 won in Korea, so owners of those cameras can start there.

## What Can You Not Do Without a Lav Input?

You cannot hide a lavalier mic under clothing. **The transmitter itself is the mic, and there is no jack to plug one into.** Put it under a shirt and you hear fabric rubbing, and on screen the transmitter shows. The black body and the DJI logo stand out too. For a wedding or a corporate interview where the mic must stay hidden, the Rode Wireless PRO is the better pick. SoundGuys recommends the older Mic 2 for commercial work.

The 400 m range is also an open-space figure. The lab figure for an interference-heavy environment is 80 m. The Mimo app draws repeated criticism as well. Settings and firmware updates run through it, and reviewers say it feels like a prompt to buy another DJI product.

The 2TX kit costs 393,000 won in Korea. **At the exchange rate of 1,334.02 won per dollar (as of 2026-11-27), the US price of $259 converts to about 346,000 won, so the Korean list price runs about 47,000 won higher.** The 1TX+1RX kit costs 225,000 won in Korea and $169 in the US (about 225,000 won), the same after conversion. ## Check Before You Buy

- ✅ You shoot vlogs, interviews, or solo videos with the transmitter clipped on
- ✅ You shoot with several cameras and want timecode to cut sync time
- ✅ You already use a DJI action camera or a Pocket camera
- ⚠️ You need to hide a lavalier mic under clothing (there is no jack at all)
- ⚠️ 32-bit float must be turned on in settings (it is off by default)
- ⚠️ Strong noise cancelling can make voices sound robotic

## My Take

No lavalier, buy the DJI. Need to hide one, buy the Rode. **For a creator who clips on and shoots, 393,000 won is money well spent.** The 16-gram weight, 32-bit float recording, timecode, and charging case match the Rode at the same price. But the missing lav input is not one line on a spec sheet. It decides how you shoot. The call is simple. Clip it on and buy it, or hide a mic and buy the Rode Wireless PRO.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need the 2TX kit if I shoot alone?**

No. The 1TX+1RX kit costs 225,000 won in Korea. If more people join later, you can add a single transmitter for 120,000 won. One receiver links up to four transmitters.

**Can I use it with only a phone?**

Yes, the receiver connects to a phone over USB-C. The transmitter pairs directly with DJI cameras over Bluetooth. If the phone is your main camera, the kit with a receiver is the easier choice.

**How do I turn on 32-bit float?**

Turn on dual-file recording in the receiver or Mimo app settings. It is off by default. If you leave it off, no 32-bit float backup file is saved.
