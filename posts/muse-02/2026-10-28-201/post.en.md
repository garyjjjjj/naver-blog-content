Open a tablet to study, and notifications get checked first. A paper notebook keeps you focused, but finding an old note later is the problem. Somewhere in between sits the color e-paper notebook. The BOOX Note Air6 C aims at that gap, but there are clear conditions to check before buying. This post is a pre-purchase introduction based on published materials, not a hands-on review.

> Key line
>
> ### **If a tablet distracts you and paper cannot be searched, a color e-paper notebook may be the answer — but check its color rendering and app compatibility first.**

![Color e-paper notebook illustration](images/01-hero.jpg)

## What makes color e-paper different?

The Note Air6 C uses a 10.3-inch Kaleido 3 color e-paper display. According to the official product page (checked 2026-10-28), black-and-white resolution is 2480×1860 (300ppi) and color is 1240×930 (150ppi). Black-and-white text looks paper-sharp, and color is shown in 4,096 hues. That means graphs in papers and color diagrams in textbooks can be told apart. But color resolution is about half the black-and-white level, so this is a reading screen for telling things apart by color, not a photo-viewing screen.

Engadget reported (2026-09-16) that the maker claims a faster processor than the Note Air 5C, improving PDF page-turn speed by 40%. That is the maker's claim; no independent measurement has been confirmed yet.

## Can it really be used for note-taking and study?

Note-taking is the center of this device. The BOOX Pen3 stylus is included, with 4,096 levels of pressure, a lasso tool that turns handwriting into text, and a calendar memo linked to schedules, all described on the official page. NeoReader, its reading app, offers color-coded highlighting, margin notes, and dark mode. Talk Insights records while you jot down key words. The official page footnote lists South Korea among supported regions and Korean among supported languages for Talk Insights, but actual Korean transcription accuracy has not been confirmed.

![Reading a paper on e-paper illustration](images/02-reading.jpg)

An illustration of reading a colorful paper and taking notes on e-paper in a library. (Illustration) Transcription accuracy may vary with the environment.

Android 16 with the Google Play Store built in also matters. Unlike a closed reader, you can install the learning apps you want. Storage is 64GB with a microSD slot, and RAM is 6GB.

## Who is it for, and who is it not for?

The table below summarizes the key facts confirmed from the official page and press coverage.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Display | 10.3-inch Kaleido 3, 300ppi black-and-white / 150ppi color |
| OS | Android 16, Google Play Store built in |
| Storage | 64GB + microSD slot, 6GB RAM |
| Battery | 3,700mAh (real-world run time not confirmed) |
| Size and weight | 225×192×5.8mm, about 440g |
| In the box | BOOX Pen3, USB-C cable, and more |
| Korean price, shipping, after-sales | Not confirmed |

![Comparing e-paper device sizes illustration](images/03-size.jpg)

An illustration comparing a 10.3-inch notebook with a smaller reader and a smartphone. (Illustration) At 440g, it weighs about as much as a paperback.

At 440g, it is a notebook for a desk or a library seat rather than a light carry. The same series offers an 8.52-inch model at about 300g, but its screen is smaller for reading papers. If you read mostly on the move, the smaller one fits; if you sit down to write and organize, the 10.3-inch model is the reference point.

Pricing on the official store varies by bundle from US$549.99 to US$656.99. Engadget reports a folio-case bundle at US$580, US$50 more than the Air 5C. The official page notes the final price is calculated at checkout based on the shipping address. Korean official pricing, shipping, and after-sales channels have not been confirmed. If you import one directly, customs, shipping, and repair routes should be weighed together.

This device suits people who read papers and textbooks with color distinctions and organize them in handwriting. If video or games are the main use, a regular tablet is better. Color e-paper trades response speed and color richness for less eye strain.
