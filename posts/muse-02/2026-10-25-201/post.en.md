Weather, schedule, and home buttons on one e-ink sheet — SwitchBot E-Ink Home Dashboard, good for a year

What if you could check today's weather and the family schedule without picking up your phone first thing in the morning? SwitchBot's "E-Ink Home Dashboard" puts weather, indoor and outdoor temperature and humidity, the family schedule, and smart home buttons on a 7.5-inch e-ink screen. It works on a desk stand or wall-mounted, and the official description says it runs up to a year without charging. This is a write-up based on published materials, not a hands-on review.

![An e-ink dashboard on a desk stand — weather cards, a schedule list, and scene buttons arranged on one screen (illustration)](images/01-hero.jpg)

## What is this dashboard?

The product is called "SwitchBot E-Ink Home Dashboard." It is an all-in-one weather station and family calendar from smart home maker SwitchBot. Its 7.5-inch black-and-white e-ink screen (800×480) shows time, weather, and schedules as cards, and four buttons on the front switch views and run scenes. The first thing to check: it is not a touchscreen.

## Why is it interesting?

**First, it is a serious weather station.** It shows indoor and outdoor temperature and humidity, a 5-day forecast, air quality, and sunrise and sunset on one screen. Up to three SwitchBot sensors add room-by-room readings, and the phone gets an alert when conditions leave a set range. Six screen themes, a front light, and AI suggestions matched to the weather and schedule are also listed as official features.

**Second, it becomes the family calendar.** It syncs with Google, iCloud, Outlook, and Yahoo, shows schedules for up to five people, and sounds reminders for things like medication, school runs, and meetings. Gizmodo reports up to 30 events per account. Hung in the entryway, it settles the "what's on today" check before anyone leaves.

**Third, one button moves the whole home.** Two front buttons switch between the weather and calendar views; the other two run smart home scenes such as lights, air conditioning, and curtains. It also offers three alarms (20, 50, and 80 dB) with a 10-minute snooze up to three times.

![An e-ink screen on an entryway wall — the family schedule arranged as a list (illustration)](images/02-calendar.jpg)

## Check before buying

This screen does not respond to touch. The front buttons are the only controls. It is also advertised as Matter-compatible, but connecting to Apple Home or Google Home needs a separate SwitchBot hub. The official hub mini bundle costs $169.99, and Gizmodo puts the hub mini alone at about $39. An outdoor meter is a separate purchase, and the "up to one year" battery depends on usage. With its built-in 5,000mAh battery it can also stay plugged in over USB-C. Official sales and shipping in Korea are unconfirmed.

## The editor's take

This fits a home that wants to look at the phone less. If the goal is checking weather and the family schedule at the door, in the kitchen, or on the desk, and killing the lights with one button before leaving, it earns its place. But there is no touch, Matter needs a hub, and sensors plus a hub can push the real spend well past the base price. It is a strong pick for a home already on SwitchBot; a home starting from scratch should total the base unit, hub, and sensors first.
