You only find out what you shot later — the 18-gram necklace camera, Kenko Pieni III

The better smartphone cameras get, the less fun taking photos feels. Shoot, check instantly on the screen, delete what you dislike, open a retouching app — the routine became too smooth. The new camera from Japanese lens maker Kenko Tokina, launched October 9, deliberately breaks the routine. There is no screen. You do not know what you captured until you transfer the files to a phone. This is a write-up based on published materials.

![A tiny film-camera-shaped camera on a neck strap — at 18 grams it wears like a necklace (illustration)](images/01-hero.jpg)

## What is this camera?

The product is called "Kenko Pieni III," the third generation of Kenko Tokina's ultra-compact toy cameras. It weighs about 18 grams (excluding memory card and accessories) and measures 53×35×18mm — the default way to carry it is on a neck strap. The body looks like a film camera but is fully digital inside. Controls are just two buttons for power and shutter, plus a photo/video switch.

## Why it is fun

**First, there is the fun of "blind shooting."** With no screen and no viewfinder, you frame by instinct and find out later. The wait itself becomes part of the pleasure, like film photography. Reviewers note that it pulls you out of the habit of checking every shot on a display.

**Second, it is light and small enough to always be on you.** At 18 grams it is lighter than four US quarters. Hang it around your neck, leave it on a shelf as decoration, grab it when something catches your eye. A magnet on the back lets you stick it to a fridge or a metal railing for selfies and hands-free shooting. It comes in four colors (Soda, Strawberry, Milk, Black), plus a separate Sanrio edition with five characters (Hello Kitty, My Melody, Kuromi, Cinnamoroll, Hangyodon).

**Third, it connects the modern way.** The third generation swapped the old micro-USB port for USB-C, so the included cable transfers photos directly to a smartphone. Files are stored on a microSDHC card (4–32GB, sold separately). A small indicator light on the back glows red for stills and blue for video.

![The magnetic back sticks the camera to a fridge — easy hands-free shooting and selfies (illustration)](images/02-magnet.jpg)

## The trade-offs

Do not expect image quality. The sensor is a 1/10-inch unit with 0.32 megapixels of effective resolution; files are interpolated in-camera to 1440×960 JPEG, with less real detail than the dimensions suggest. Kenko's own sample photos show flowers turning into patches of vivid color and foliage softening into watercolor. Video records at 720×480 and 30fps with audio — far from a current smartphone. The 1.8mm f/2.8 lens (about 42mm equivalent) is fixed focus and struggles closer than 30cm, ISO is fixed at 100 with auto shutter, and exposure is left entirely to the camera. The memory card is sold separately.

## The editor's call

This is not a camera for shooting well — it is a camera for shooting with joy. It suits people bored by how uniform smartphone photos look, anyone who wants a camera that lives in a bag at all times, or parents looking for a first camera for a kid. But if you must capture a decisive moment reliably, keep it as a side camera only. At ¥6,600 the price is not cheap for a toy camera, but it makes sense if you are buying the experience of shooting without a screen. Note the Sanrio edition costs more at ¥8,470.
