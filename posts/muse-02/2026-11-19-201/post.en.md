DJI Osmo Nano vs Insta360 GO Ultra: Tiny 4K Cameras, 61 Dollars Apart

If you want a pocketable 4K camera, the choice comes down to two products: the DJI Osmo Nano and the Insta360 GO Ultra. Both are magnetic modules around 50 grams that snap into a dock with a screen, so they look like twins at first glance. The split comes down to storage, screen, and price. This post was put together from official material and independent comparisons and user reports.

## What Are These Cameras?

Both products split into a camera module and a dock with a screen. Wear the module alone, clipped on with a magnet, and shoot hands-free. Snap it into the dock and it works like a normal action camera with a screen.

**The modules weigh 52 grams against 53 grams, practically the same.** Both shoot 4K at 60 frames per second, and each camera alone is waterproof to 10 meters.

## What Changes When You Clip One to a Hat?

Clip one to a cap brim or a collar with a magnet and you record a first-person view while using both hands.

Magnetic mounting means **no separate mount is needed,** and voice commands and gestures can also start recording.

In a side-by-side test under the same conditions, the GO Ultra kept more detail and contrast, while the Nano came out darker in some scenes. Colors looked more natural from the Nano, according to the same test. In auto mode, **the GO Ultra pleases more easily.** In bright daylight, though, both deliver sharp video with little difference.

## Where Screen and Storage Split Them

The GO Ultra Action Pod has a 2.5-inch flip screen, so you can check your framing for selfies right away. The Nano Vision Dock screen is 1.96 inches and small, and for a selfie you have to detach the camera, turn it around, and reattach it. **The GO Ultra leads on selfie convenience.**

The Nano records straight to 64GB or 128GB of built-in memory, and a microSD card only takes files off through the dock. The GO Ultra has no built-in memory and records directly to a microSD card.

**Swapping cards removes capacity worry.** The card is a separate purchase, though, so its price adds to the first bill.

The Nano records 90 minutes on its own, and the GO Ultra 70 minutes. Docked, both reach 200 minutes (1080p).

If you shoot without the dock on a long day out, **the Nano is the better pick.** Charging is faster on the GO Ultra, reaching 80 percent in 12 minutes.

The Nano shoots in 10-bit D-Log M, which leaves room for grading, and it offers 4K slow motion at 120 frames per second.

The GO Ultra shoots 8-bit Active HDR, which looks good right away but leaves less room to grade. **Grade it, pick the Nano. Share it, pick the GO Ultra.** Note that the Nano turns stabilization off in 4K slow motion.

## What to Weigh Before Buying

User reports say heat builds up when 4K runs for a long time in such a small body. Long 4K takes may stop before the stated runtime. If continuous 4K shooting is the goal, **keep heat in mind for both products.**

Once built-in memory fills up, you have to connect the dock and clear files before shooting again. Swapping cards does not help. **On a trip of several days, clear the files every night.**

The GO Ultra weak points are the card cost and close focus. No card is included, so one must be bought separately, and user reports say close-up shots turn soft. The minimum focus distance is 30 cm, so **for vlogs shot at arm's length, check the focus distance first.**

The Nano is not sold through the DJI online store in the United States and goes through Amazon and other retailers. In Korea, the DJI store sells it normally, so **buyers in Korea can ignore this.**

## Price and Availability in Korea

By US retail prices, the screen-equipped kits are $389 for the Nano and $449.99 for the GO Ultra, a gap of about 61 dollars. The Nano body alone starts at $299, but without a screen it is awkward to use, so **compare kits with screens.**

In Korea, the Nano Standard Combo starts at 389,000 KRW at the DJI store. The GO Ultra Standard Bundle sells in the 570,000 KRW range on Coupang, though prices vary a lot by seller. The Creator Bundle is 583,000 KRW. **Even standard to standard, the gap is around 180,000 KRW.**

Converted at the 2026-11-18 rate, the Nano is about 520,000 KRW and the GO Ultra about 600,000 KRW. That is a straight conversion with shipping and import duty left out.

## My Take

If swappable cards and a big screen matter, pick the GO Ultra. If built-in storage and grading matter, pick the Nano. Either way, **both are magnetic cameras around 50 grams.**

- ✅ Storage: decide whether you will swap cards or live with built-in memory
- ✅ Screen: if you shoot a lot of selfies, pick the 2.5-inch flip screen
- ✅ Budget: check the 61-dollar gap between screen-equipped kits

Settle the storage question first, and one of the two will drop out on its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**I own a GO 3S. Do I need to switch?**

The GO 3S shoots 4K at 30 frames per second, has built-in storage, and could be bought as a body alone. The GO Ultra changes that to 4K at 60 frames per second, a larger sensor, and longer battery life. If 4K at 60 frames per second is not needed, the GO 3S is enough.

**Can I connect an external microphone?**

The Nano connects to DJI microphones, but the first-generation DJI Mic cannot connect directly. The GO Ultra supports Bluetooth and Type-C microphones. A Type-C microphone only works while the camera sits inside the Action Pod.

**Can I shoot underwater?**

Each camera alone is waterproof to 10 meters. The dock and pod are only IPX4 splash-resistant, so take the camera off for underwater shots.
