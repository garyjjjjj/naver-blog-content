## What Is This Telescope?

Hestia is a smartphone telescope from Vaonis of France. It launched in 2024 and is now on regular sale, and it won a CES 2024 Innovation Award. Put your phone on top of the device and the phone camera becomes the telescope camera. **It has no power supply and no battery: it is a purely optical device.** Six lenses and prisms gather light and send it to the phone camera sensor.

It measures 17 x 24 x 5.5 cm and weighs 850 g, about the size of a book. Set it on a tripod and hold the phone in place with magnets, and you are ready.

## What Changes When Your Phone Camera Gets 25x?

The Moon changes. A Moon that was a white dot becomes a photo showing craters and the dark lunar plains. According to the maker, **sharpness improves up to 5 times**. A 30 mm aperture gathers the light, and 25x magnification puts the Moon large inside a 1.8-degree field of view.

The app helps with the shot. The Gravity by Vaonis app analyzes the stars to work out where you are pointing and guides you to your target. According to the maker, it stacks several short exposures to produce **the effect of up to a 30-second exposure**.

## Why It Needs No Power

There are no electronics inside Hestia at all, so there is nothing to charge and nothing to update. **A better phone means better photos**, according to the maker. When you change phones, the camera gets better while the telescope body stays the same. It is water-resistant and impact-resistant, so it can be used outdoors.

There is a drawback, though. It has no automatic tracking, so it does not follow the stars. Long exposures for nebulae and galaxies are therefore limited. **The Moon and the Sun are its home turf**, according to independent reviews.

## How Far Can You Shoot?

The Moon works. Craters and the lunar plains show up in photos. The Sun can be photographed only with the dedicated solar filter. Without a filter, the Sun can damage your eyes and the phone camera, so **a solar filter is essential**. Bright deep-sky objects are in the catalog too, but without tracking, only bright targets are within reach.

Light pollution matters as well. An Outside Online hands-on report says that in downtown Toronto, the app could not find the six stars it needs for its star analysis. Out in the countryside, the Moon photos came out well. **A dark sky is a precondition.**

It works in daylight too. Scenery mode pulls distant landscapes and wildlife closer. One hands-on report says it zoomed in on a shoreline a mile (about 1.6 km) away and picked out a canoe.

## My Take

It suits someone who wants to try Moon photography without committing to a full telescope. All you need is a phone, and there is no power to worry about. **As a first step into astrophotography**, the mid-300,000-won price fits.

If you want to shoot nebulae and galaxies seriously, an automatic tracking telescope is the better choice. Check three things.

- ✅ Phone compatibility: check whether your phone meets the Sun and Moon conditions, and whether you need a higher-end model for deep sky
- ✅ Sky conditions: in a city with light pollution, the star analysis may struggle, so a dark sky is needed
- ✅ No tracking: it does not follow the stars, so long-exposure deep-sky shots are limited

If you want to start lightly with Moon photos, it is worth buying. If you are aiming for deep sky, choose an automatic tracking telescope instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can it photograph the Sun?**

Yes, with the dedicated solar filter. Without a filter, the Sun can damage your eyes and the phone camera. The solar filter comes in the Ultimate Pack and the Solar Pack.

**Does my phone work with it?**

Sun and Moon shooting works on iPhone 8 or later and Android 11 or later. Deep-sky shooting targets iPhone 13 or later. Galaxy S20, S21 Ultra, S22 Ultra, S23 Ultra, and Pixel 5, 6, 7 also work. The Gravity app can check Android compatibility.

**Is a tripod essential?**

At 25x magnification, hand shake shows up clearly, so a tripod is effectively essential. The Standard Pack and above include one, while the Essential Pack is the device only, so you need to bring your own.
