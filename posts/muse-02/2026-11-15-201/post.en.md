Fairphone Gen. 6 | A Phone That Lasts Until 2033, Check the Repair Costs First

> Problem — When a phone breaks, you never know what the repair will cost, so you start shopping for a new one.
>
> Bottom line — **The parts prices are public. A battery costs €39.95, and the phone is supported until 2033.**
>
> Next step — Start with the Gen. 6+ as the default, and check how to get one to Korea first.

When a smartphone breaks, there are two options: pay an expensive repair bill, or buy a new phone. The problem is that you cannot know the repair cost in advance. The Fairphone Gen. 6, made by the Dutch company Fairphone, offers a third answer: swap only the broken part, and receive software and parts until 2033.**

This article is based on official material, independent reviews, and teardown reports. The Gen. 6 arrived in June 2025. The Gen. 6+ followed in August 2026.

## What is this phone?

It is a 6.31-inch smartphone **built to be repaired**. Its display is a 6.31-inch LTPO OLED. The resolution is 1116×2484, and the refresh rate adapts from 10 to 120Hz. The Gen. 6 runs on a Snapdragon 7s Gen 3, the Gen. 6+ on a Snapdragon 7s Gen 4.

Memory is 8GB on the Gen. 6 and 12GB on the Gen. 6+. Both models have 256GB of storage, expandable to 2TB with a microSD card (official product page). There is a physical switch on the side for 'Moments' mode, which cuts down notifications.

## Why is swapping parts so easy?

**It opens with one screwdriver, and there is no adhesive.** With a single T5 Torx screwdriver you can open the back and replace 12 parts yourself, including the battery, display, USB-C port, cameras and speaker. The battery and display use no adhesive, so no heat gun is needed to take them out.

The teardown specialists at iFixit gave this phone a **perfect 10 out of 10 for repairability** (iFixit teardown). Spare part prices are published openly in the official store: a battery is €39.95 and a display is €89.95. A USB-C port is €19.95 (official parts store). One exception: the mainboard is not a user-replaceable part, so you cannot upgrade the CPU or RAM later.

## The long-life promise is the support period

**Software and spare parts are supported until 2033.** Fairphone says it will support software and spare parts until 2033 (official product page). The Gen. 6 comes with at least seven Android upgrades and a five-year warranty (registration required for the extended warranty).

The battery is rated for 1,000 charge cycles. Fairphone says it will still hold more than 80% of its capacity after 1,000 charges.

## Gen. 6 vs Gen. 6+

**The only differences are the chip and the memory.** The display, battery, cameras and support period are the same (official comparison page). In Europe the Gen. 6 costs €599 and the Gen. 6+ €649 (official product page). The difference is €50.

If you plan to use the phone for eight years, 12GB of RAM leaves more headroom. Notebookcheck's review questioned whether 8GB of RAM will stay sufficient across the long support period. If the €50 difference is what you are weighing, the Gen. 6+ is the safer pick, while the Gen. 6 is worth considering when it appears as an official refurbished unit (from €549).

## Where are the limits?

**The compromises are in four areas.** First, the cameras: a 50MP main camera with OIS and a 13MP ultra-wide. The front camera is 32MP.

Notebookcheck's review found the main camera's colors natural, but dynamic range and the ultra-wide disappointing.

Second, the performance ceiling. The mid-range chip is plenty for everyday use, but demanding games are not this phone's territory.

Third, water resistance. The **IP55** rating handles splashes, but the phone cannot be submerged.

Fourth, charging and connectivity. There is no wireless charging, USB-C data runs at USB 2.0 speeds, there is no headphone jack, and the charger is sold separately.

## Price and buying from Korea

**The European list price starts at €599, but getting one to Korea is the bigger condition.** The official European prices are €599 for the Gen. 6 and €649 for the Gen. 6+. At the 2026-11-14 exchange rate of about 1,475 KRW per euro, that converts to roughly 880,000 KRW for the Gen. 6. The Gen. 6+ comes to about 960,000 KRW.

Shipping and customs are not included, so the real purchase price will be higher.

Buying from Korea is the biggest condition. Fairphone ships directly only to Europe and the United States. **You cannot order from the official store with a Korean address**; Fairphone's own support page points to ordering through a friend or family member in Europe or the US, or through a global reseller (Fairphone support).

Outside Europe and the US there is no repair or replacement support, and the five-year extended warranty cannot be registered outside Europe. Check compatibility with Korean mobile networks separately before buying.

## My take

The Fairphone Gen. 6 suits people who want to use a phone for a long time, fix it when it breaks, and solve battery worries by swapping the battery. **What you are buying is certainty: repair costs turn from an unknown bill into a published parts price.** It is not for people who put the camera and performance first, or who take domestic after-sales service for granted.

If you buy one, the order is set: start with the Gen. 6+ as the default, compare how much cheaper the Gen. 6 gets as a refurbished or discounted unit, and check the route to Korea, customs, and network compatibility before deciding.
