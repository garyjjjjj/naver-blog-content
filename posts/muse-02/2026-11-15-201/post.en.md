A Smartphone Built to Last Until 2033 — Fairphone Gen. 6 and Gen. 6+, What to Check Before Buying

When a smartphone breaks, there are two options: pay an expensive repair bill, or buy a new phone. The Fairphone Gen. 6, made by the Dutch company Fairphone, offers a third answer: swap only the broken part, and receive software and parts support until 2033. The Gen. 6 arrived in June 2025, followed by the Gen. 6+ in August 2026 with a faster chip and more memory.

## What is this phone?

The Fairphone Gen. 6 is a 6.31-inch smartphone from the Dutch company Fairphone. Its display is a 6.31-inch LTPO OLED, 1116×2484, with an adaptive 10–120Hz refresh rate. The Gen. 6 runs on a Snapdragon 7s Gen 3, the Gen. 6+ on a Snapdragon 7s Gen 4. Memory is 8GB and 12GB, and both models have 256GB of storage, expandable to 2TB with a microSD card (official product page, checked 2026-11-15). There is a physical switch on the side for 'Moments' mode, which cuts down notifications.

## Just swap the parts

This phone is held together by screws. With a single T5 Torx screwdriver you can open the back and replace 12 parts yourself, including the battery, display, USB-C port, cameras and speaker. The battery and display use no adhesive, so no heat gun is needed to take them out. The teardown specialists at iFixit gave this phone a perfect 10 out of 10 for repairability (teardown published 2026-11-14). Spare part prices are published openly in the official store: battery €39.95, display €89.95, USB-C port €19.95 (official parts store, checked 2026-11-15). One exception: the mainboard is not a user-replaceable part, so you cannot upgrade the CPU or RAM later.

## The long-life promise is the support period

Fairphone says it will support software and spare parts until 2033 (official product page, checked 2026-11-15). The Gen. 6 comes with at least seven Android upgrades and a five-year warranty (registration required for the extended warranty). The battery is rated for 1,000 charge cycles, and Fairphone says it will still hold more than 80% of its capacity after 1,000 charges.

## Gen. 6 vs Gen. 6+: what's different?

The only differences between the two models are the chip and the memory. According to the official comparison page, the display, battery, cameras and support period are the same. The Gen. 6+ has a Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 and 12GB of RAM; the Gen. 6 has a Snapdragon 7s Gen 3 and 8GB. In Europe the Gen. 6 costs €599 and the Gen. 6+ €649, a €50 difference (official product page, checked 2026-11-15). If you plan to use the phone for eight years, 12GB of RAM leaves more headroom. Notebookcheck's review questioned whether 8GB of RAM will stay sufficient across the long support period. If the €50 difference is what you are weighing, the Gen. 6+ is the safer pick, while the Gen. 6 is worth considering when it appears as an official refurbished unit (from €549).

## Drawbacks and things to check

First, the cameras: a 50MP main camera with OIS, a 13MP ultra-wide and a 32MP front camera. Notebookcheck's review found the main camera's colors natural, but dynamic range and the ultra-wide disappointing. There is no telephoto lens.

Second, the performance ceiling. The mid-range chip is plenty for everyday use, but demanding games are not this phone's territory.

Third, water resistance. The IP55 rating handles splashes, but the phone cannot be submerged.

Fourth, charging and connectivity. There is no wireless charging, USB-C data runs at USB 2.0 speeds, there is no headphone jack, and the charger is sold separately.

## Price and buying from Korea

The official European prices are €599 for the Gen. 6 and €649 for the Gen. 6+ (checked 2026-11-15). At the 2026-11-14 exchange rate of about 1,475 KRW per euro, that is roughly 880,000 KRW and 960,000 KRW. Shipping and customs are not included, so the real purchase price will be higher.

Buying from Korea is the biggest condition. Fairphone ships directly only to Europe and the United States. You cannot order from the official store with a Korean address; Fairphone's own support page points to ordering through a friend or family member in Europe or the US, or through a global reseller (Fairphone support, checked 2026-11-15). Outside Europe and the US there is no repair or replacement support, and the five-year extended warranty cannot be registered outside Europe. Check compatibility with Korean mobile networks separately before buying.

## My take

The Fairphone Gen. 6 suits people who want to use a phone for a long time, fix it when it breaks, and solve battery worries by swapping the battery. What you are buying is certainty: repair costs turn from an unknown bill into a published parts price. It is not for people who put the camera and performance first, or who take domestic after-sales service for granted. If you buy one, the order is set: start with the Gen. 6+ as the default, compare how much cheaper the Gen. 6 gets as a refurbished or discounted unit, and check the route to Korea, customs, and network compatibility before deciding.
