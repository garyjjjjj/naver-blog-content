Your logo on the mic — Insta360 Mic, a wireless mic with a screen

When levels clip during an interview, that take is gone. Speak too loudly and the audio tears; speak too softly and all you get is room noise. The new wireless mic launched yesterday (October 22) tackles the problem inside the hardware: slot in a microSD card and the transmitter keeps its own 32-bit float recording as backup. This is a write-up based on published materials.

![A channel logo shown on the E-Ink screen on the front of the transmitter — the image stays even when powered off (illustration)](images/01-hero.jpg)

## What is this mic?

The product is called "Insta360 Mic," launched October 22, 2026 by Insta360, the action-camera maker. It sits between the flagship Mic Pro and the entry-level Mic Air. The standout feature is the 1.22-inch color E-Ink display on the front of the transmitter. You upload any image through the app — a channel logo, an emoji, a photo of your dog — and it stays on the mic. Because it is E-Ink, the image remains even when the power is off and stays readable in direct sunlight.

## Why it is fun

**First, the transmitter is its own backup recorder.** With a microSD card slot, inserting a card starts internal recording on the transmitter. It supports 32-bit float, which captures a far wider dynamic range than conventional recording, so audio that came in too loud or too quiet can be recovered in post. If the wireless link drops or the camera recording fails, the transmitter keeps a copy — a safety net for moments you cannot reshoot, like wedding vows. Engadget called it "getting the Pro's internal storage for the price of a microSD card."

**Second, the battery and connectivity are practical.** The manufacturer rates the transmitter at up to 13 hours per charge, 34 hours with the charging case, and says 5 minutes of charging gives about 2 hours and 20 minutes of use (manufacturer testing). Insta360's own cameras (X6, X5, Luna Ultra, Luna Pro, GO Ultra, Ace Pro 2) can pair directly over Bluetooth through "Direct Connect," no receiver needed.

**Third, multi-mic setups stay flexible.** One receiver pairs with up to four transmitters, and Mic Pro transmitters can join the same setup. Two transmitters can also broadcast to four receivers at once, which fits multi-camera shoots that want the same audio baked into every angle. An AI chip powers noise reduction, automatic gain control (anti-clipping mode and level-evening mode), and three voice tone presets are included.

![Insert a microSD card and the transmitter records its own 32-bit float copy — a file survives even when the wireless link drops (illustration)](images/02-recording.jpg)

## The trade-offs

The lower price comes with cuts. Unlike the Mic Pro's 32GB of built-in storage, there is none — you buy the card separately. The three-capsule pickup-pattern selection is gone too, leaving a single omnidirectional capsule. There is no 3.5mm input jack on the transmitter, so an external lavalier needs a separate USB-C-to-3.5mm adapter ($5). The receiver dongle has been described as chunky. At 19.4 grams the transmitter itself is light.

## The editor's call

This is not a mic you buy for sound quality alone — it is gear designed around the whole shoot. Solo YouTubers and people who often record multi-person interviews will feel the E-Ink customization and the 32-bit float backup. If you already own a wireless mic and rarely lose a take, consider that the 3.5mm input adapter costs extra before switching. The dual kit at $249.99 sits at a similar level to the rival DJI Mic 3, so the deciding question is whether the E-Ink customization and expandable storage speak to you.
