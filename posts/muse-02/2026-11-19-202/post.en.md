Framework Wireless Touchpad Keyboard, a Couch All-in-One for $99

Using a PC hooked up to the TV from the sofa means picking up the keyboard and the mouse in turn. The Framework Wireless Touchpad Keyboard, which opened for pre-orders on November 17, folds the keyboard and the touchpad into a single board and removes that hassle. It costs $99. The first batch ships in Q1 2027. This post was put together from the official announcement, related coverage, and a hands-on look at a prototype from April.

## What Is This Keyboard?

The Framework Wireless Touchpad Keyboard is a wireless keyboard with a touchpad on the right side of the keys. It targets devices where a mouse has nowhere sensible to sit, like a PC hooked up to a living-room TV, a server, or a Raspberry Pi.

**The keyboard module comes straight from the Framework Laptop 12.** Built with Lite-On, the scissor-switch module has 1.5 mm of key travel and 19 mm key spacing. The touchpad measures 68.8 by 85.6 mm, clicks, and tracks at 120 Hz. The board is 357 by 118.7 by 16.4 mm and weighs 484.9 grams, with a 0.8 mm steel plate inside.

## What Changes on the Sofa?

Set the single board on your lap and typing and cursor work continue in the same posture.

**It stores profiles for up to four devices and switches between them with one press.** The USB-A dongle rides in a slot at the back of the board, pre-paired on channel 1. Three Bluetooth 6.0 channels and a wired USB-C connection cover everything from a PC to a BIOS screen on one board. Wireless range is listed at up to 10 meters, enough for a living room.

## How Are the Battery and Repairs Different?

The battery in this keyboard can be swapped. The 1,200 mAh cell connects through a standard connector, so a screwdriver is all it takes to replace it.

**A worn-out battery does not mean throwing the whole keyboard away.** Framework says it lasts over a year with occasional use and over a month with a few hours of use a day. Those are the maker's claims, so treat them as reference figures until independent tests appear.

The firmware is open too. It is an open-source fork of ZMK, and keys can be remapped from the browser. Surface CAD files are published on GitHub under CC BY 4.0, so accessories can be printed at home.

## Compared With the K400 Plus

Framework built this keyboard with a clear target: the Logitech K400 Plus, the default living-room keyboard.

| Model | Key figures | Pick it when |
|---|---|---|
| Framework Touchpad Keyboard | $99, 120 Hz touchpad, replaceable battery | You want to keep it for years and fix it yourself |
| Logitech K400 Plus | $34.99, small touchpad, built-in battery | You need a living-room keyboard cheaply, right now |

The price is about three times the K400 Plus at list price. **The gap comes from the touchpad size, the replaceable battery, and the open firmware.** The K400 Plus sells for around $29 on Amazon, so it still wins on value. If the small touchpad never bothered you, the K400 Plus is enough.

## What to Check Before Buying

Start with the fact that this is a pre-order. The first batch ships in Q1 2027, and no exact delivery date is set. Coverage on November 18 also showed early batches marked as sold out, so check availability on the checkout page.

**The official announcement says nothing about a backlight.** If you often type in a dark living room, buy with the backlight marked as unconfirmed. The launch layout is US English ANSI only.

There are no independent reviews yet, since nothing has shipped. Battery life and wireless stability exist only as the maker's figures for now. The $50 deposit is fully refundable.

## Price and Buying in Korea

The US list price of $99 converts to about 132,000 KRW. That is a straight conversion with shipping, customs, and VAT left out. The $50 deposit is about 67,000 KRW, and the rest is charged shortly before shipping.

Buying from Korea means ordering from abroad. **Framework does not ship directly to Korea.** Orders go through a US or European delivery address, which adds shipping fees and customs steps. There is no news of an official Korea launch yet.

## My Take

If you often use a living-room PC and the K400 Plus touchpad feels cramped, this one is worth waiting for. **The replaceable battery and open firmware are what the $99 pays for.**

- ✅ Living-room PC: when you want to use a PC from the sofa without a mouse
- ✅ Long-term use: when a replaceable battery and a repairable build matter
- ✅ Price: check whether three times the K400 Plus is worth it to you

Buy the K400 Plus if you need one now, and consider the pre-order if you want one board to keep for years.

## FAQ

**Does it work with a Mac?**

Hold Fn and O for three seconds to switch to Mac Mode, and Fn and P to switch back. Framework says most macOS shortcuts and multi-finger gestures are supported.

**Is there a Korean layout?**

The launch version comes in US English ANSI only. There is no Korean keycap printing, and other language layouts are under survey. Korean input works through the operating system's input method.

**My K400 Plus still works. Do I need to switch?**

No, if the touchpad size and the built-in battery never bothered you. The time to switch is when the K400 Plus breaks or when you set up a living-room PC from scratch. At $99, this is an expensive keyboard.
