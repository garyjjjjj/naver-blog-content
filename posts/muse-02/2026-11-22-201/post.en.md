## What Is SlashVibe?

The SlashVibe V1 is a desk controller for AI agents. **One set pairs a body with three buttons and a joystick under a small screen, plus a wireless controller you hold in your hand.** The screen shows each agent as running, done, or waiting for your approval. When an agent stops, the screen updates and plays a short alert.

It does not replace your computer. **The body stays on the desk, and the agent work keeps running on your computer.** SlashVibe only shows progress, takes your approval, and passes along voice instructions. Co-founder Loda used to make keyboards, and his campaign letter says he started this product looking for a way to type less, not to type more comfortably.

The campaign raised more than 1,500% of its $10,000 goal, from over 600 backers. Kickstarter also picked it as a Project We Love.

## Why Gather Agent Status on a Small Screen?

Its job is to cut down the number of windows you open. Run three agents and your screen fills with windows fast. Flipping between them to check which one finished breaks the thought you were holding. SlashVibe moves that check to a small screen beside your desk. One glance is enough.

Approvals happen on the screen too. **When an agent asks for permission, you approve it or pick another option with the controller buttons.** If you need to read the details, you bring that window to the front on your computer. You stop discovering an hour later that an agent was waiting for you.

Meeting notes appear on the screen as well. It shows live notes from Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams meetings, and takes down to-dos as you speak. Spoken to-dos can move into agent tasks. Meeting-note accuracy has no hands-on verification yet. This is a pre-shipment product, so campaign materials are all the reviews there are.

## What Changes When You Work by Voice?

```blog

```

**Press the button and recording starts within 100 milliseconds.** There is no wait for it to get ready, so a thought goes straight into words. What you say does not land as a raw transcript. It drops filler words and tidies the sentence into text. The guide says prompts come out precise, emails polished, and notes natural.

Hold the controller close to your mouth and you can speak in a near-whisper. **You can give instructions in an open office or late at night without raising your voice.** There is no need to press send before you speak. When you finish, you choose send or cancel. A misspoken line never reaches the agent as it was.

Korean is on the supported language list. The official guide lists English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean, among others. It adds that accuracy varies by language. There is no verified material yet on how well Korean voice input actually works.

## How Open Is the Open Source?

```blog

caption: "The code will be released under AGPL-3.0 before shipping. There is no public repository yet. (Illustration)"

```

**Four layers go open source: the desktop app, the device firmware, the communication protocol, and the SDK.** The code will be released under the GNU AGPL-3.0 license before devices ship. You can read the code to see how your data is handled, and attach features of your own. Hardware design files and the official backend services stay closed.

The SDK works in Node.js and Python. **You do not need to touch circuits or flash firmware. Code running on your computer appears on the screen.** The campaign shows examples: a screen that tracks API spending against a monthly budget, a countdown to a deadline, and a pixel pet that perks up as tasks finish. There is a mini game too.

Stream Deck and macro pads point in a different direction. **A Stream Deck maps someone else's software shortcuts to buttons, while SlashVibe hands its screen, keys, joystick, and voice to your code.** It is closer to a small screen you draw on yourself than a button box. That difference may not register for someone who does not write code.

## What Should You Check Before Backing?

**First, Korea shipping and its cost.** Country-by-country shipping is listed in the campaign risks. This research could not confirm whether Korea is covered. Shipping may not be included in the pledge, so check the shipping guide on the Kickstarter page before backing.

**Second, the paid voice plan price.** The free plan covers the device and a usage limit for cloud dictation. Heavier use needs a paid plan, and its price will be announced before shipping. AI tool subscriptions and API usage are paid separately to the accounts you already use.

**Third, whether the code-release promise holds.** The license is named as AGPL-3.0 on the campaign page. But there is no public repository yet. If you want to read the code before buying, it is better to watch for the release before shipping and then decide.

Then there is the delivery schedule. **The first batch is expected to start shipping in March 2027, but FCC certification and parts supply could push it back.** Backing on Kickstarter is not a purchase. Dates can move and rewards can run late. Ask first whether you can wait about four months.

This product suits people who run several coding agents at once. If you want to see agent status from your desk, give instructions by voice, and build with the code yourself, $189 is a try worth making. If you run one agent, or switch windows without trouble, there is no strong reason to buy one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How is backing on Kickstarter different from buying?**

Once the goal is passed, the backing stands and payment is taken when the campaign closes. But rewards are not guaranteed, and dates and specifications can change. This campaign already passed its $10,000 goal.

**Does voice input work offline?**

You can set up a local speech-to-text service yourself. Cloud dictation is covered within the free plan's usage limit, and heavier use needs a paid plan.

**When do I pick a color?**

Black and Silver are the two finishes, and you pick one in the survey after the campaign. You do not choose a color at the backing step.
