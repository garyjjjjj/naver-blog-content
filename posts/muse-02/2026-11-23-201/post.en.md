BOOX Picco: A Card-Sized E-Reader for $99 — Worth It?

The BOOX Picco is a card-sized 3.97-inch e-reader, and the official price starts at $99.99. It is built for the minutes on a commute or in a queue, when you would rather reach for a chapter than your phone. The catch is that books need converting before they go on, and PDF files do not open. This post pulls together the official materials and independent coverage.

## What Is the BOOX Picco?

BOOX opened pre-orders on September 28, 2026, for a pocket e-reader. It is the first product in a new line called BOOX Tiles, and it packs a 3.97-inch monochrome e-ink screen into a card-sized body. **It measures 105.5 x 62 x 5.5 mm and weighs about 58 g.** It does not stick to a phone by itself. The included magnetic ring case snaps it to the back of a phone so the two travel as one. The screen works by touch and by physical page-turn buttons, and the front light adjusts between warm and cool tones.

## Can You Actually Read on a 3.97-Inch Screen?

This is not a screen for settling into a long novel. It is a screen for turning a few pages at a time. A page holds little text, so you turn pages often. **At 58 g, though, it is easy to hold in one hand for a long time, and e-ink is gentler on the eyes.** The front light runs from 2,800 K to 6,300 K, so it works at night too. In 9to5Google's hands-on, it lasted about a week reading one to two hours a day. The screen rotates four ways, for left or right hand, portrait or landscape. Small tools are built in too: a Pomodoro timer, a to-do list, and a countdown.

## Why Is Getting Books Onto It So Awkward?

You cannot just copy a book file over and open it. Books go through the BOOX Tiles app or website, which converts them first. **An EPUB file does not open directly on the device; it is converted to the OPUB format and then transferred.** PDF files are not supported. Whether Korean e-book files work, and how Korean text displays, is unconfirmed.

The iOS version of the BOOX Tiles app was only available through TestFlight at hands-on time. Wi-Fi is 2.4 GHz only, and one hands-on report had trouble connecting through a mesh network. There is no dictionary yet. That is workable if you keep a few books on it at a time, but it is the wrong device for moving a whole library over.

## How Does It Compare With the Xteink X4 Pro?

The closest rival is the Xteink X4 Pro. Its screen is 0.33 inches larger, and its battery is bigger at 1,100 mAh. **The price gap is under a dollar, but the charging is different.** The X4 Pro charges through pogo pins, so it needs its own cable, while the Picco takes a plain USB-C cable.

The Picco has the sharper screen, 235 PPI against 219 PPI, and it is 14 g lighter. The X4 Pro opens EPUB files without converting them, while the Picco needs the conversion step. The X4 Pro also made TIME's list of the best inventions of 2026, which is worth noting. Either way, both are 4-inch-class screens, so both suit short reading sessions rather than long novels.

## Price and Buying From Korea

The official store price depends on the warehouse. **The Hong Kong warehouse lists $99.99, and the US warehouse lists $109.99.** At the exchange rate of 1,346.05 won per dollar, that is about 135,000 won and about 148,000 won. Shipping and customs are not included, so the final cost is higher. Amazon US had it in stock at $109.99. Whether BOOX sells it officially in Korea, and what shipping to Korea costs, is unconfirmed. Korean e-book file compatibility is unconfirmed too, so check which file formats the BOOX Tiles app accepts before buying.

## My Take

If you want to build a habit of reading in spare minutes, the Picco fits. At 58 g it slips into a pocket without thought, and with no notifications, it is easier to stay with a book than on a phone. **Still, the book conversion step and the lack of PDF support are things to accept before buying.** If you want EPUB files to open without converting, look at the X4 Pro first. If you want a full reader that also runs apps, the Palma 2 is the better match.

- ✅ You keep picking up your phone and sliding into short video
- ✅ You have short books to read on the commute or while waiting
- ✅ A 6-inch reader feels heavy to you
- ⚠️ PDF files, comics, or long novels as the main use do not fit
- ⚠️ If you want books to go on without converting, this is not it

The choice comes down to use. Spare-minute reading points to the Picco, and EPUB files without converting point to the X4 Pro.

## FAQ

**Can it open PDF files?**

No, PDF is not supported. The formats the BOOX Tiles app and website can convert are text e-books such as EPUB, MOBI, and AZW3. If you need PDF, look at a 6-inch reader or larger.

**Is the iPhone app officially released?**

At hands-on time, the iOS version of the BOOX Tiles app was only available through TestFlight. Whether it has reached the App Store is unconfirmed.
