DJI Neo 2: Palm-Sized Drone, Is 295,000 Won Worth It?

A drone that lifts off from your palm is useless if it cannot dodge obstacles — one selfie can end with a broken aircraft. The DJI Neo 2 puts a forward-facing LiDAR on a 151g body. ## What Is This Drone?

The DJI Neo 2 is a follow-me camera drone that takes off from your palm and returns to it. It launched in China on October 30, 2026, and went on sale globally on November 11, 2026, including in the DJI Korea store. It weighs 151g and measures 147 x 171 x 41mm, small enough for a pocket.

**The biggest change from the Neo 1 is obstacle sensing.** The Neo 1 only looked down, while the Neo 2 watches in every direction with a forward LiDAR, a downward infrared sensor, and a monocular vision system. The propellers sit inside guards, so fingers are less at risk.

## Does It Really Lift Off From Your Palm — and Come Back?

It takes off and lands using only your palm, with no controller. Press the side button and it lifts off; switch on palm return and the drone flies back above your hand.

**It also answers to gestures.** Raise a fist to activate it, spread your hand to move it left, right, up, or down, and show a flat palm to bring it back for landing. Voice control works too, but only in English and Chinese. Pair it with an Apple Watch and you can watch a live view on your wrist and fly it by voice.

A small display on the front shows the current shooting mode at a glance. When you fly with gestures and voice alone, that little screen turns out to be genuinely useful. When you run or ride a bike, ActiveTrack follows at up to 12 m/s, faster than the Neo 1's 8 m/s, and a new Cycling mode is included.

## How Well Does It Dodge Obstacles?

The forward LiDAR sees obstacles even in the dark. DPReview reports that the Neo 2 senses obstacles in every direction in real time and steers around them. The Neo 1 had no obstacle sensing at all, so it hit trees and walls easily.

**It also stands up to wind better.** The Neo 1 was rated Level 4 (20-28 km/h), while the Neo 2 is rated Level 5 (up to 38 km/h), according to the maker. A long-term review notes that where the Neo 1 wobbled in strong gusts, the Neo 2 stays far steadier.

But sensing is not magic. One independent review describes the drone missing a thin pole, hitting it, and falling into water. The official guidance also says thin branches and wires may go undetected. **Open space is the default place to fly.**

## How Much Better Is the Video?

The 12MP 1/2-inch sensor gets a brighter f/2.2 aperture, up from f/2.8, and the gimbal grows from one axis to two. 4K moves from 30fps to 60fps, slow motion reaches 4K 100fps, and vertical video improves from 1080p to 2.7K.

**Storage more than doubles to 49GB.** The Neo 1 had 22GB. But there is no microSD slot, so once it fills up you have to offload footage. The maker says 49GB holds about 105 minutes of 4K/60fps video, or about 175 minutes at 4K/30fps.

| Neo 1 | 135g, no sensing, 4K 30fps, 22GB, 171,800 won | You want the cheapest way into a palm drone |

The figures come from the DJI store comparison table. **Safety and video quality are the categories the Neo 2 wins.** The price gap is 123,200 won. If you want to stop worrying about losing the aircraft to an obstacle, that gap is worth it. If you only fly slowly in an open field, **the Neo 1 is enough.**

One more thing: Neo 1 batteries do not fit the Neo 2. If you own a Neo 1, you cannot carry the batteries over.

## What to Check Before Buying

**Flight time comes first.** The official maximum is 19 minutes, but it drops to 17 minutes with the propeller guards on, and a long-term real-world review measured around 14 minutes. One battery will not cover a long session. The Fly More Combo's three batteries are close to essential.

Charging matters too. Charging through the aircraft takes about 70 minutes, while the charging hub fills three batteries at once in about 68 minutes. **No charger is included.** Only a USB-C cable comes in the box, so you need a 65W PD charger of your own to reach hub charging speeds.

Where you fly matters as well. **At 151g, registration and a pilot license are not required in Korea**, but the basic rules still apply: no-fly zones, a 150m altitude ceiling, and no night flying. You cannot fly over crowds, roads, or near airports. Check the Drone One-Stop site or the Ready to Fly app for restricted zones before you fly.

**Buying from the US is not an option.** According to DPReview, the Neo 2 is not officially sold in the United States. Importing one is not an official route.

Gesture control has conditions too. It works only 2-5m from the subject in bright light, and the official guidance says not to use it in front of thin objects or plain walls.

## Price and Buying in Korea

The drone-only package is 295,000 won, the Fly More Combo (Drone Only) is 395,000 won, the Fly More Combo is 493,000 won, and the Motion Fly More Combo is 731,000 won. **The Fly More Combo is the store bestseller.** It includes the RC-N3 controller, the Digital Transceiver, three batteries, and a charging hub.

**Decide first whether you need a controller.** If you only use your palm and gestures, the drone-only package is enough. If you want to fly far or use tracking at full speed, you need the RC-N3, which extends transmission range from 500m over Wi-Fi to 10km.

## My Take

If this is your first palm drone and you want to worry less about obstacles, the Neo 2 fits. **The 295,000 won buys the LiDAR and 4K 100fps.**

- ✅ Running and riding footage: when it follows you with no controller
- ✅ Family outings: when it lifts off from your palm for a group photo
- ✅ Check first: battery count, charging time, and the flight rules where you will fly

If you already own a Neo 1, there is little reason to switch. Unless obstacle sensing and better video matter a lot to you, keep flying the Neo 1.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use Neo 1 batteries in the Neo 2?**

No. Battery capacity changed from 1,435mAh to 1,606mAh, and the two models' batteries are not compatible. If you switch from a Neo 1, you need new batteries.

**What is the Digital Transceiver?**

It is the O4 transmission module used when connecting a controller. With it attached, the drone weighs 160g. It comes with the Fly More Combo but not with the drone-only package. If you buy the drone alone and add a controller later, you need the transceiver too.

**Do I need to buy a charger separately?**

Only a USB-C cable comes with the drone-only package. To charge three batteries at once in about 68 minutes through the hub, you need a 65W PD charger. If you do not have one at home, buy it together.
