Retro Song Bird: Game Boy Music Speaker, 3 Things to Check Before Backing

If you want to hear Game Boy music, you have to turn on the console and play the game. Retro Song Bird is a speaker that plays the music of a game as soon as you plug in its cartridge. ## What Is This Speaker?

Retro Song Bird is a speaker that plays music from Game Boy and Game Boy Color cartridges. It is the first product from Bird Cartel, a one-person project by B.J. Malicoat, and its Kickstarter backing opened in mid-November. It is not a device for playing games. It is built only for playing music. It has no screen or controller.

The maker compares it to a cassette tape. Plug in a cartridge and the music starts, and buttons change the track and the volume. More than 1,900 games are supported.

## What Happens When You Plug In a Cartridge?

Plug in a cartridge and every track and sound effect from that game plays. Inside is an emulation that copies the Game Boy sound chip, reading only the music part of the original cartridge ROM. According to the maker, it reproduces the sound at register level.

Sound comes out three ways: the built-in speaker, a 3.5 mm line-out, and a USB-C output that splits the four sound channels. Over USB-C, each channel can be sent separately to a DAW, so chiptune makers can work with the original instrument sounds one by one.

## It Does More Than Play Music

It also works as an archive tool. Connect it to a computer and you can back up or restore a cartridge save file and copy the whole ROM. Tracks and sound effects can be exported as WAV files. The maker says over-the-air updates will add supported games and features.

Plug in a Game Boy Camera and it becomes a USB webcam. At 128×112 pixels and about 5 frames per second, it is closer to a joke than a meeting tool. Alarm mode wakes you with music from a cartridge of your choice. Press the button to snooze, pull the cartridge to stop.

## What to Weigh Before Backing

The biggest condition is that this is not a product on sale yet. It is a backing stage. This is the maker's first crowdfunding campaign, and press reports say the current prototype looks very different from the planned production unit. The April 2027 shipping date is a target, not a guarantee. A pledge is closer to production support than a purchase price.

Second is whether you own cartridges. Playing music needs a Game Boy cartridge. If you own none, you have to buy cartridges first. One cartridge only plays the music from that game.

Third is shipping to Korea. Shipping to Europe is confirmed, but shipping to Korea, shipping cost, and import duty are not confirmed. The retail price after the campaign is not set yet either.

There are no independent reviews yet. The prototype stage means no press measurements or user reports exist. The sound has to be judged on the maker's claims and press coverage alone.

## Price and Availability in Korea

The Kickstarter price is $79 for one unit and $150 for a two-pack. The $59 early-bird price is already sold out. Converted, $79 is about 105,000 KRW, but that leaves out shipping and import duty. As of November 16, backers number more than 420. Pledges passed $45,000, more than three times the $15,000 goal.

The campaign ends on December 15. No domestic release plan in Korea is confirmed. Check the shipping terms on the campaign page before backing.

## My Take

It suits people with cartridges asleep in a desk drawer best. If you want to hear the music without playing the game, it removes the hassle of turning on the console. For chiptune makers, the split channel output is practical.

If you own no cartridges, YouTube or streaming is cheaper and faster for music alone. If the backing risk feels heavy, waiting for retail makes more sense. Three things to check:

- ✅ Cartridges: count how many Game Boy cartridges you own
- ✅ Backing risk: first campaign, the production unit may differ from the prototype, and April 2027 is a target date
- ✅ Shipping to Korea: check shipping cost, import duty, and whether Korea is covered on the campaign page

If you own cartridges, it is worth backing. If you do not, waiting for retail is the better move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you play games on it?**

No. It plays music only, with no screen and no gameplay. The maker's website separately offers playing games in a browser.

**Can you use it without cartridges?**

Not for listening to music. A cartridge is required. You can also use it for homebrew by putting GB Studio music on a writable cartridge and playing it.

**Can you back it from Korea?**

A Kickstarter account is all you need to back it. Shipping to Korea, shipping cost, and import duty still need checking on the campaign page.
