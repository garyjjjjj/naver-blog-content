You can't see your photos — a camera that develops after 24 shots, Await

Thousands of photos pile up in your phone's gallery — how many do you ever open again? Today's camera flips that problem around. It has no screen. You can't see a photo right after taking it. Only after 24 shots is the "roll" developed, and real prints are delivered to your home. A camera that sells waiting as the product. This introduction is based on published materials.

![A tiny screenless camera — deliberately leaving a gap between shooting and seeing (illustration)](images/01-hero.jpg)

## What is this camera?

It's called "Await," a pocket digital camera from Swiss electronics company Rolling Square (known for its inCharge cables), unveiled at CES in January 2026. It packs an 8MP Sony IMX179 sensor, a fixed f/2.0 lens, an optical viewfinder, a xenon flash and a 10-second self-timer. There is no screen. The only thing on the body is a small OLED counter showing how many shots are left.

> One line
>
> ### **Shoot, forget, receive later — waiting is this camera's feature.**

## Why it's fun

**First, the "24 shots per roll" rule.** Like a film camera, a roll is complete only at 24 shots. Once finished, it syncs to the app automatically and gets the retro filter — developed from a study of vintage photographs — plus an orange date stamp. The body holds up to three rolls (72 shots); syncing empties it. Without the habit of checking and deleting every shot, you start thinking once more before pressing the shutter.

**Second, real prints arrive at your door.** When a roll is done, twenty-four 6×4-inch physical photographs are shipped. In automatic mode, after a 24-hour grace period each roll prints at $8.99 plus shipping; in manual mode you pick which rolls to print at $9.99 plus shipping. You pay only when something is printed. Want to skip the filter? Connect over USB-C to a PC and pull the original files untouched.

![Fill 24 shots and the roll develops in the app — then 24 real prints ship home (illustration)](images/02-roll.jpg)

**Third, early hands-on impressions are good.** Reviewers who received pre-production units ahead of Kickstarter say it "works better than it has any right to." The entire product is the gap between the moment and the memory — and apparently that gap genuinely creates feeling. There are just two buttons (power/shutter, flash/self-timer), so there's no menu to get lost in.

![Two buttons, one viewfinder — too simple to get lost in a menu (illustration)](images/03-prints.jpg)

## The catch

It's still crowdfunding, so it's not officially out yet. Shipping is targeted for December 2026, but crowdfunding timelines slip all the time. 8 megapixels is modest next to today's phone cameras, and printing costs about $9 per roll plus shipping as a running cost. Shipping availability and fees to Korea need checking on the campaign page. Whether "waiting" feels like fun or a chore will differ by person.

## Editor's take

This camera doesn't fight the megapixel war. As the film-camera trend shows, people are starting to miss "the feeling of inconvenience." Instead of shooting and checking instantly, you forget — then receive. It's for people who want to capture a family trip or a child's everyday moments and hold the real thing. But printing costs keep adding up, and there's the usual crowdfunding schedule risk. If you feel digital fatigue, one "slow camera" might beat deleting your phone's camera app.
