When a laptop breaks, you usually face two options: pay an expensive repair bill or buy a whole new laptop. The Framework Laptop 13 offers a **third option**. You swap out only the broken part, and when the CPU feels slow, you replace just the mainboard and keep using it. A new model with AMD Ryzen AI 300 series chips arrived in February 2025. ## What is this laptop?

The Framework Laptop 13 is a 13.5-inch notebook from Framework Computer in the United States. The 2025 model runs on **AMD Ryzen AI 300 series** chips. You can choose from three CPUs: the Ryzen AI 5 340, AI 7 350, and AI 9 HX 370.

The display is a 13.5-inch 3:2 panel. A **120 Hz high-refresh display** is also available. At 15.95 mm thick and under 1.3 kg, it is easy to carry in a bag.

## Why do the ports snap in like cards?

The ports on this laptop are not fixed in place. Four slots take Expansion Cards — USB-C, USB-A, HDMI, headphone jack — so you put the port you want where you want it. **Four ports are decided by four cards**, not by the chassis.

If you need HDMI today and two USB-C charging ports tomorrow, you pull the cards out and swap them. The cards are sold individually, so you carry fewer adapters. **At least one USB-C card** is needed for charging.

## Does swapping the mainboard really mean a new CPU?

Yes. The mainboard, with the CPU on it, is itself a replaceable part, so when a new CPU generation arrives, you **buy a new mainboard** instead of a new laptop.

Memory and storage are user-replaceable too. DDR5-5600 memory goes up to 96 GB and M.2 SSDs up to 8 TB. Mainboards for the 2025 model started at $499.

Teardown specialist iFixit gave the Framework Laptop a **10/10 repairability score**. Replaceable parts are the core of the design.

## Does the battery last a full day?

It depends on the test. Framework describes the 61 Wh battery as lasting "all day", yet Tom's Hardware titled its review "Increased performance at the expense of battery life." The **61 Wh battery's "all-day" claim comes from the manufacturer**.

Expert Reviews measured 10 hours 7 minutes in its benchmark. Light everyday use came to about 8 hours. One Linux user recorded about 6 hours 30 minutes. That is a weak spot for anyone who works a full day away from an outlet.

There is also a performance ceiling. With **integrated graphics only and no discrete GPU**, it is enough for documents, the web, and light coding, but not the place for demanding games or heavy video editing.

The cheapest DIY Edition has a catch. **It ships without memory, storage, or an operating system**, so you buy the parts, assemble them yourself, and Windows costs extra. That may be a hassle if you want a laptop that just works out of the box.

The successor is worth weighing too. The Laptop 13 Pro, announced in April 2026, has a larger 74 Wh battery, a touchscreen, and a haptic touchpad. Buying the 2025 model now may mean buying the previous generation. One caveat: reports say a memory price surge pushed the Pro's DIY price up to start at $1,199.

## Price and availability in Korea

The official US price starts at $899 for the DIY Edition and $1,099 for the pre-built version. Memory, storage, and Windows push the **real price higher**. A one-year limited warranty is included. A three-year extended warranty costs $139 more.

| Option | Starting price | Pick it when |
|---|---|---|
| DIY Edition | $899 | You want to choose the parts and assemble them yourself |
| Pre-built | $1,099 | You want to switch it on and use it right away |
| Laptop 13 Pro DIY | $1,199 (reported) | You need the bigger battery and touchscreen |

Availability in Korea is the biggest condition. **Official direct shipping to Korea is still unconfirmed.** Buyers in Korea will likely need an international order or a forwarding service, and should check duties, shipping costs, and local after-sales support separately.
