A Laptop You Repair by Swapping Parts — Framework Laptop 13: What to Check Before Buying

When a laptop breaks, you usually face two options: pay an expensive repair bill or buy a whole new laptop. The Framework Laptop 13 offers a third option. You swap out only the broken part, and when the CPU feels slow, you replace just the mainboard and keep using it. A new model with AMD Ryzen AI 300 series chips arrived in February 2025.

## What is this laptop?

The Framework Laptop 13 is a 13.5-inch notebook from Framework Computer in the United States. The 2025 model runs on AMD Ryzen AI 300 series chips. You can choose from three CPUs: the Ryzen AI 5 340, AI 7 350, and AI 9 HX 370 (Framework official product page, checked 2026-11-14). The display is a 13.5-inch 3:2 IPS panel, either 2256×1504 at 60 Hz or 2880×1920 at 120 Hz. It is 15.95 mm thick and weighs under 1.3 kg (Framework official product page, checked 2026-11-14).

## The ports snap in like cards

The ports on this laptop are not fixed in place. Four slots take Expansion Cards — USB-C, USB-A, HDMI, headphone jack — so you put the port you want where you want it. If you need HDMI today and two USB-C charging ports tomorrow, you pull the cards out and swap them. The cards are sold individually, so you carry fewer adapters. At least one USB-C card is needed for charging (Framework official configurator page, checked 2026-11-14).

## Swap the mainboard, get a new CPU

Memory and storage are user-replaceable. DDR5-5600 memory goes up to 96 GB and M.2 SSDs up to 8 TB (Framework official product page, checked 2026-11-14). The bigger difference is the mainboard. The mainboard, with the CPU on it, is itself a replaceable part, so when a new CPU generation arrives, you buy a new mainboard instead of a new laptop. Mainboards for the 2025 model started at $499 (Notebookcheck, 2025-02-25). Teardown specialist iFixit gave the Framework Laptop a 10/10 repairability score.

## Shortcomings and things to check

Battery life comes first. Framework describes the 61 Wh battery as lasting "all day" (manufacturer claim). Yet Tom's Hardware titled its review "Increased performance at the expense of battery life." Expert Reviews measured 10 hours 7 minutes in its benchmark and about 8 hours in light everyday use. One Linux user recorded about 6 hours 30 minutes.

Second is the performance ceiling. There is no discrete graphics card, only integrated graphics. That is enough for documents, the web, and light coding, but not the place for demanding games or heavy video editing.

Third is the DIY catch. The cheapest DIY Edition ships without memory, storage, or an operating system. You buy the parts, assemble them yourself, and Windows costs extra. That may be a hassle if you want a laptop that just works out of the box.

Fourth is the successor. The Laptop 13 Pro, announced in April 2026, has a larger 74 Wh battery, a touchscreen, and a haptic touchpad. Buying the 2025 model now may mean buying the previous generation. One caveat: reports say a memory price surge pushed the Pro's DIY price up to start at $1,199 (PCWorld, 2026).

## Price and availability in Korea

The official US price starts at $899 for the DIY Edition and $1,099 for the pre-built version (at the February 2025 launch, checked 2026-11-14). Memory, storage, and Windows push the real price higher. A one-year limited warranty is included, and a three-year extended warranty costs $139 more (Framework official configurator page, checked 2026-11-14).

Availability in Korea is the biggest condition. As of July 2024, Framework did not ship directly to Korea (Framework community post, 2024-07-16). As of 2026-11-14, I could not confirm whether Korea is on the official list of shipping countries. Buyers in Korea will likely need an international order or a forwarding service, and should check duties, shipping costs, and local after-sales support separately.

## My verdict

The Framework Laptop 13 suits people who want to keep a laptop for years, fix it when it breaks, and upgrade only the CPU later. The swappable-part design is itself the reason to buy it. It does not suit people who need a full day away from an outlet, who do heavy gaming or video work, or who want a laptop that just works out of the box. If you buy one, the order is fixed: pick the pre-built or DIY version that fits you, add up the real cost with memory, storage, and Windows, check the shipping route and duties to Korea, and compare it with the newer Pro model.
