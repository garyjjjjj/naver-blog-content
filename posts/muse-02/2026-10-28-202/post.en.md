How many times a day do you reach sideways from typing to grab the mouse? The Keychron Nape Pro tries to remove that motion altogether. Instead of a mouse beside the keyboard, a small trackball sits in the empty space in front of it, and your thumb moves the cursor. This post is a pre-purchase introduction based on published materials, not a hands-on review.

> Key line
>
> ### **Moving the cursor without lifting your hands off the keyboard — that is the one thing this trackball changes.**

![Trackball in front of a keyboard illustration](images/01-hero.jpg)

An illustration of a small trackball device placed below a keyboard spacebar. (Illustration) Its actual size fits the free space in front of a keyboard.

## The space in front of the keyboard becomes an input area

The Nape Pro is designed to sit right below the spacebar. According to the official product page (checked 2026-10-28), it has a 25mm trackball, six buttons, an adjustable control dial, and OctaShift placement in eight directions. It can lie flat, stand vertically between split keyboard halves, or sit diagonally. The design is meant to work for left-handed users as well.

Connectivity covers 2.4GHz wireless, Bluetooth, and USB-C wired modes. The sensor is PixArt 3222 with a 1K polling rate. Based on ZMK, its buttons and macros can be changed in the Keychron Launcher web app, and settings are saved on the device so they carry across computers. Silent clicks are another point the official page highlights.

Unveiled at CES 2026, the device drew first impressions from The Gadgeteer (2026-01-20) that the ball rolls smoothly and the buttons feel satisfying. Those are show-floor impressions, not a long-term test. The official page itself is candid about a short learning curve before it feels natural. If a trackball is new to you, a few awkward days should be expected.

## What else can it do?

The six buttons can hold more than clicks: shortcuts such as copy, paste, undo, and app switching, plus multi-step macros. The dial can scroll, zoom, scrub timelines, or adjust volume. For flipping through presentation slides or moving along a video editing timeline, work can be handled without leaving the keyboard.

![Rolling a trackball with a thumb illustration](images/02-thumb.jpg)

An illustration of moving a cursor with a thumb while keeping a typing posture. (Illustration) Less hand travel is the key benefit.

It is not, however, a gaming device. The Gadgeteer article also notes it does not fit gaming scenarios where absolute precision and high polling rates matter. A 1K trackball is aimed at documents and general office work. And while less wrist movement is the design intent, that is not a verified medical benefit.

Price and stock must be checked in their latest state. The official page (checked 2026-10-28) lists the price at US$89.99, but both black and white variants are out of stock. The same page notes that second-batch preorders are now shipping, with all orders expected to be dispatched by the end of October. That means an order placed now may not arrive right away. Korean official sellers, pricing, and shipping have not been confirmed.

This product suits people whose keyboard is the center of their work: writing, organizing material, and using shortcuts often. If you already love your mouse or trackball, or gaming precision matters, there is little reason to switch. As Keychron's first trackball, it is also worth checking the latest firmware state before buying, since first-generation hardware is often refined through updates.
