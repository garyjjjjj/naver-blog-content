Amazfit Balance 2: A $219.99 Training Watch, Worth It Over the XT?

The Amazfit Balance 2 is a training smartwatch rated for up to 21 days of typical use, and the US official store now lists it at $219.99. It launched at $299.99. It pairs a sapphire screen with a 45 m diving certification and dual-band GPS. This post pulls together the official materials and independent coverage.

## What Is the Amazfit Balance 2?

Amazfit released this multisport smartwatch worldwide on June 24, 2025. It puts a 1.5-inch AMOLED screen behind sapphire glass, in an aluminum-frame body rated 10 ATM with a 45 m diving certification. **It measures 47.4 x 47.4 x 12.3 mm and weighs 43 g without the strap.** It supports more than 180 sports modes, maps for 40,000 golf courses, and downloadable offline maps. Its place is as the one watch for running, swimming, golf, HYROX, and diving.

## How Long Does the 21-Day Battery Really Last?

The official 21 days assume all-day wear, heart rate measured every five minutes, 90 minutes of GPS running a week, the screen raised 100 times a day, and 150 messages a day. **Hands-on reports land between about one week and two weeks, depending on settings.** TechRadar saw about a week with the always-on display and frequent GPS workouts, while the5krunner landed close to two weeks on its own settings. Lifestack put real-world use at about 15 days. GearBrain found the 21-day claim held up well in practice. Whatever number you land on, it is a large change from charging an Apple Watch every day.

GPS recording time is generous too. The official figure of up to 33 hours in accurate mode was confirmed past 30 hours in the5krunner's testing. Battery is rarely the first worry on a long run or a full-day hike. **The always-on display, though, eats the battery fast.** One owner report had only eight to nine days with it switched on. To get close to 21 days, use raise-to-wake instead.

## Is Its Training Tracking as Trustworthy as Garmin?

For running and cycling, reviewers found it close to a chest strap. In Wareable's testing, average and maximum heart rate during interval running and indoor cycling came close to a Garmin HRM-600 chest strap. **A 13.1-mile loop came within 0.08 miles of a Garmin Forerunner 965.** Consumer Reports also rated its heart rate, blood oxygen, and GPS accuracy highly. The BioTracker 6.0 sensor tracks heart rate, sleep, stress, and sleep heart-rate variability around the clock.

The weak spot is clear too. Heart rate lags during movements that twist the wrist, such as weightlifting. Run to the Finish found the heart rate results less impressive, and owner reports mention the same lag in interval workouts. **If exact interval heart rate matters, pair a Bluetooth chest strap.** The Balance 2 connects to Garmin and Polar chest straps without trouble, according to Wareable's testing.

## Balance 2, XT, or Balance 3?

With three watches in the same line, the choice comes down to a $20 gap and a $150 gap. The XT costs $199.99 and keeps the same screen, battery, and GPS, but the glass drops to mineral, water resistance falls to 5 ATM with no diving, the golf app is gone, storage shrinks to 4 GB, and there is one speaker. **For $20 more, the Balance 2 adds sapphire, diving, golf, 32 GB, and dual speakers.** At current prices the $20 gap is small, so if you use even one of sapphire or diving, the Balance 2 is the better pick.

The Balance 3 starts at $369.99, a $150 step up. The screen grows to 51.4 mm and 3,000 nits, and it adds a flashlight, four buttons, and 41 hours of GPS. It is also bigger and heavier. For training records, the Balance 2 is already enough. Look at the 3 only when you need the larger screen and flashlight.

| Model | Key figures | Pick it when |
|---|---|---|
| Balance 2 XT | $199.99, mineral glass, 5 ATM, 4 GB storage | You never dive or play golf and want the lowest price |
| Balance 2 | $219.99, sapphire, 10 ATM, 45 m diving, 32 GB storage | You use sapphire, diving, or golf |
| Balance 3 | From $369.99, 51.4 mm, 3,000 nits, flashlight, 41-hour GPS | You need the bigger screen and flashlight |

## Price and Buying

The US official store lists it at $219.99. It launched at $299.99, and records show it at $249.99 in August 2026. **At 1,342.22 won per dollar, that is about 295,000 won.** Shipping and taxes are not included, so a direct order costs more in the end. The XT is $199.99, about 268,000 won. The Balance 3 starts at $369.99, about 497,000 won. US prices change often, so check again right before paying.

In Korea, Amazfit's official mall sells it. Naver listed a 338,000 won hot deal on October 28, 2026, and Danawa's lowest price was 371,070 won as of November 18. The lowest recorded price was in the 320,000 won range on Gmarket. **The Korean official mall list price could not be confirmed from its product page.** Compare the official mall, Naver, and Danawa before buying. Korean buyer reviews praise the battery, but one notes that Korean voice support is unavailable. The Zepp Flow voice assistant varies by country, so Korean may not work. Call quick replies are Android-only, and camera control is iOS-only.

## What to Check Before Buying

Know that the app ecosystem is narrow. This is not Wear OS, so you cannot add Google Maps or a wide range of apps to the watch. Core features work without a subscription, but there is a separate paid option, Zepp Aura, at $69.99 a year. **Owner reports also describe firmware updates that drained the battery within hours or deleted workout records.** Those cases followed the July 2026 update, so check recent reports before updating right after purchase. There is no LTE version, and contactless payment support in Korea is unconfirmed. If you want to pay from the watch alone, this is not the one.

- ✅ You hate daily charging and want a watch for training records
- ✅ You want one watch for running, swimming, golf, and HYROX
- ✅ You would take sapphire and diving for a $20 difference
- ⚠️ It is not for you if the app ecosystem and watch payments matter
- ⚠️ If firmware stability worries you, wait and check reports first

The decision comes down to one question. If you use diving, golf, or sapphire, buy the Balance 2. If you use none of them, buy the XT.

## My Take

If you are tired of charging every day and training records are the point, the Balance 2 fits at this price. At $219.99, it is only $20 above the XT and adds sapphire, diving, golf, and 32 GB. **Just do not expect an app ecosystem, Korean voice, or payments.** It is not the watch for someone who wants an Apple Watch or Galaxy Watch that handles daily life too. For training alone, few options at this price combine GPS, battery, and screen like this one.

## FAQ

**Does it work with an iPhone?**

Yes. It supports iOS 15.0 and above and pairs through the Zepp app. Call quick replies are Android-only, though, and camera control is iOS-only, so features split by phone.

**Is the Zepp Aura subscription required?**

No. Core features work without a subscription, and the press release says no additional subscription is required. Zepp Aura is a $69.99-a-year optional extra.

**How do I care for it after swimming?**

The official specs state 10 ATM water resistance and a 45 m diving certification, but they do not give after-swim cleaning steps. Check the official manual for care instructions.
