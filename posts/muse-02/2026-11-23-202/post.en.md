Pixl Nub: A Robot That Locks Your Phone, Worth Backing at $249?

The Pixl Nub is a small robot that sits on your desk, locks your phone, and measures your focus time. The Kickstarter Super Early Bird price is $249, with delivery planned for July 2027. The catch is that the headline feature, app locking, does not work yet on the pre-production unit. This post pulls together the official materials and an independent hands-on review.

## What is the Pixl Nub?

The Pixl Nub is a palm-sized desk robot from Pixl Robotics. **It is about 13 cm tall, with no screen and no camera.** Place it on your desk and plug it in, and it senses when you sit down and starts a focus session. When the session ends, it shows three numbers: how long you focused, how many times you were interrupted, and how fast you got back after each interruption. It does not talk at you; it turns its head and chirps to get your attention.

## Can radar really measure focus?

It reads you with radar, not a camera. **The 60 GHz radar measures heart rate and breathing from the small movements of your chest, while the 24 GHz radar watches whether you are seated and whether you got up.** The sensing range is about 0.4 to 1.5 meters while you sit still. No face photos and no voice recordings are kept. In the maker's words, you can track your heart-rate trend without wearing anything.

A reviewer who used the pre-production unit for two weeks summed up accuracy this way. He never compared it side by side with a medical device, and the numbers wobble when you move. **The direction matters more than the number itself.** It is a tool for watching trends: are you working calmly, or are you fidgeting? He also noted it is not a fit for people who work on a walking pad. The maker states clearly that it is not a medical device.

## Does the phone lock work right now?

Not yet. That is the biggest gap in the pre-production unit. The Kickstarter page and the official FAQ call app locking the feature most backers come for. During a focus session it locks TikTok and Instagram while leaving Gmail, Slack, and Claude open. You can choose which apps to lock, and they open again when the session ends.

But the app on the reviewer's pre-production unit had no lock setting at all. He started a session, opened TikTok and Instagram, and they opened without a problem. The tabs for interruption counts and refocus time showed only sample data. **What Nub actually does today is run a Pomodoro timer and show a live heart-rate and breathing chart.** Pick 25, 45, or 60 minutes and the phone shows the time left, heart rate, breathing rate, and a live graph. At the end of a session you get a summary with focus time, peak heart rate, breathing rate, and movement share.

The maker promises to fill the gap before shipping, so whether it closes is something to watch. The reviewer emailed the maker and had no reply by the time he recorded. Backing now means paying for what it promises to do at shipping, not for what it does today.

## How is it different from other desk robots?

Nub is not the first small robot on a desk. **Its fork in the road is measurement, not cuteness.** Other robots focus on dancing and talking; Nub focuses on showing your focus time as numbers. The table below is based on the maker's comparison page (vendor prices as of October 2026).

| Device | Key figures | Pick it when |
|---|---|---|
| Pixl Nub | $249, radar focus measurement, no subscription, ships July 2027 | You want your focus time as numbers |
| Eilik | $169.99, expressive robot, no subscription | You want a cute desk companion |
| EMO | $279, voice assistant, face recognition, no subscription | You want a talking companion robot |
| Loona | $499, moves on wheels, face recognition, no subscription | You want a robot that roams your desk |

On price alone, Nub sits in the middle. It has no camera, and the pre-production unit has no microphone either. Not filming your face or listening to your voice is its sharpest split from the others. The trade-offs are that its headline feature is not ready yet and shipping is eight months away. The pre-production finish was rough too, with visible 3D-print marks and discoloration, and the reviewer could hear the motors. The maker says the shipping version will look better.

## Price and backing terms

The Kickstarter campaign started on November 4, 2026 and ends on December 4. The cheapest price available now is the Super Early Bird at $249. At the exchange rate of 1,346.1 KRW per dollar (Finnhub), that is about 335,000 KRW. Shipping and tax are extra, so the final amount is higher. The next tier, Early Bird, is $299 (about 402,000 KRW), and the retail price after the campaign is $399 (about 537,000 KRW). The VIP tier for reservation holders is $200 (about 269,000 KRW), with shipping and tax included.

Whether it ships to Korea has not been confirmed. Shipping cost and customs for Korea are also unconfirmed. The official FAQ says shipping and tax are calculated at Kickstarter checkout based on your address. **Kickstarter is a place to back a project, not a shop.** If the campaign misses its goal, backers are not charged; even if it funds, delivery of rewards is not guaranteed. Shipping is planned to start in July 2027. Hardware crowdfunding often runs late, so treat that date as a target, not a promise.

## My verdict

For people whose focus is worth money, Nub makes a case. **The reviewer worked it out as five hours of focus at $50 an hour to pay back $249.** That math holds if you work for yourself or your output feeds your income. For a fixed salary, it can turn into a $249 desk toy. I would not rush in before there is confirmation that the app lock will be in the shipping version. Right now Nub is a Pomodoro timer with a heart-rate chart on top.

- ✅ You work alone and your phone keeps breaking your flow
- ✅ You want your focus time as numbers and want to change the habit
- ✅ You want a desk device with no camera and no microphone
- ⚠️ If you want a finished product with the app lock working, wait for hands-on reports after shipping
- ⚠️ If motor noise bothers you, or you do not want to wait until July 2027, it is not a fit

The call splits on one line. Back it if you believe Nub will pay back five hours of focus; wait for the finished product if you do not.

## FAQ

**Does the app lock work on Android phones too?**

The hands-on review covered iPhone only, and Android support has not been confirmed. Check the Kickstarter page for Android app support before backing.

**What happens if the campaign misses its goal after I back it?**

Kickstarter only charges backers if the campaign reaches its funding goal. If it falls short, no one is charged and the project does not go ahead. Even when a campaign funds, reward delivery is not guaranteed.

**Can I trust the heart-rate numbers as they are?**

There is no side-by-side validation against a medical device. The hands-on verdict is that the trend matters more than the exact number. For health tracking, look at a wearable or a medical device instead.
