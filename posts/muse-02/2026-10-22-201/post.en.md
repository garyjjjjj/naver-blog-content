Don't lift your hands off the keys — a keyboard whose key surfaces are the trackpad, Clevetura S

You're typing, and your right hand leaves the keyboard to find the mouse. Grab the mouse, come back to the keyboard. A few centimeters of travel, repeated hundreds of times a day. Today's keyboard removes that trip entirely. Press a key and a letter is typed; swipe across the key surfaces and the cursor moves. This introduction is based on published materials.

![Press to type, swipe to move the cursor — your hands never leave the keyboard (illustration)](images/01-hero.jpg)

## What is this keyboard?

It's called the "Clevetura S" (CLVX S), a 78-key compact keyboard built on Clevetura's "TouchOnKeys" technology. A low-profile keyboard with scissor switches (the laptop style) on an aluminum base. The key surfaces are capacitive touch sensors: press to type, swipe for gestures, and the keyboard tells the two apart on its own. According to the manufacturer, AI recognizes where both hands are and filters out accidental touches.

>
> ### **Your hand never travels to the mouse — everything ends on the keyboard.**

## Why it's fun

**First, your hands never leave the keyboard.** Cursor movement, scrolling, zooming, clicking, even macOS gestures are handled on the key surfaces. The touch area can be set to the left or right side of the keyboard, whichever feels natural. Reading a document, scrolling, jumping the cursor around code, then typing again — the flow never breaks.

**Second, there are gesture sliders.** Swiping left or right across the top row adjusts volume or rewinds video. The manufacturer's app lets you set per-app presets for Photoshop, Figma, Premiere and others, mapping frequent actions to a single finger swipe.

![Swipe the top row to adjust volume — hand frequent actions to a single finger (illustration)](images/02-gesture.jpg)

**Third, it travels well.** At 430 grams and under 29 cm wide, it packs three-device Bluetooth plus USB-C. It shines where there's no room for a mouse — a cafe, a train. Reviewers agree it "shines when space is tight and scrolling or clicking keeps interrupting typing."

![No room for a mouse on this narrow desk — one small keyboard does it all, 430 grams (illustration)](images/03-desk.jpg)

## The catch

There's a learning curve. If you rest your fingers on the home row, you may get inputs you didn't want. Reviewers agree that "tuning the gesture sensitivity matters." Battery life is up to 44 hours (about 10 days' worth) without backlight per the manufacturer, but real-world reviews call it "below expectations." A mouse is still better for precise drawing or long pointer sessions. The layout is U.S. (ANSI) only, and $199 is more than double the price of an ordinary compact keyboard.

## Editor's take

This keyboard doesn't replace the mouse. It fits people whose work constantly hops between typing and navigating — documents, code reviews, reference-hopping. If you work at a narrow desk or on the move, it's an idea worth trying once. But keep the mouse for precise work and let the keyboard handle only what it can finish on its own. If accidental inputs worry you, check the sensitivity adjustment first.
