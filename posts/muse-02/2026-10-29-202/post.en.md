A film roll sits on the desk. You pick it up and realize it is not a film roll at all — it is a camera. The Analog Capsule Camera is a digital camera shaped like a roll of 35mm film. It stores photos on microSD instead of film and charges over USB-C. It costs $55. But this camera is not for taking better photos. It is for buying the fun of shooting lightly. This post is a pre-purchase introduction based on published materials, not a hands-on review.

>

## What is inside the film-roll camera?

According to the sales page (checked 2026-10-29), the camera has an ABS resin body, measures about 4.5 × 4.7cm, and weighs about 25g (excluding microSD). The sensor is a 1-megapixel CMOS, the lens is 3.2mm, and the minimum focusing distance is about 20cm. Its small 80×160-pixel display is mostly for checking a shot right after taking it. Photos are JPEG, video is 1920×1080 (30fps) MJPEG AVI, and storage is microSDHC up to 32GB (FAT32). The 230mAh rechargeable battery charges in about 120 minutes and runs for about 60 minutes (without flash). The box includes a USB cable, a strap and a manual.

One point must be stated clearly. The sensor is 1 megapixel, but the still-image resolution is listed as 3760×2128 — roughly 8-megapixel size. Whether upscaling or interpolation is involved is not explained on the sales page, so it is unconfirmed. Do not judge image quality from that number alone. The sales page argues the opposite virtue: grain, softness and blown highlights are analog character, not flaws. That is a matter of taste, not a claim of good image quality.

## How is it fun to use?

Its use comes from its weight and size. At 25g, it can hang from a bag like a charm. With the included strap, you can leave it dangling and grab it the moment something catches your eye. On a desk, it works as a small object in its own right. The film-roll-style box adds to the effect, so part of the product is the surprise when you hand it to someone as a gift.

![Mini camera hanging from a bag illustration](images/02-charm.jpg)

An illustration of a tiny camera hanging from a bag like a charm. (Illustration) At 25g, it is light enough to carry all day.

The limits are just as clear. About one hour of battery life does not suit long shooting sessions. The tiny screen makes proper review difficult, and low-light performance is not confirmed on the sales page. Whether it has a flash is also not stated in the spec list, so it is unconfirmed. Whether a microSD card is included is not stated either, so buying one separately may be necessary.

## What to check before buying

The price is $55 on the sales page, with both color options shown as available. A Yanko Design article (2026-10-09) introduces the product, sold through Yanko Design's select shop. Its shipping page says orders ship from a Tokyo warehouse and that international customers bear customs duties and taxes, but it does not state Korea-specific conditions. So Korea delivery and the final checkout total must be checked at checkout. Official Korean sales, local service and warranty terms are not confirmed.

One more honest limit: independent hands-on reviews of this exact product are hard to find so far. The Yanko article and outside media summarizing it are the main sources, and they are not independent measurements. Reports on similar film-roll cameras (such as the OPT100 Neo Film) exist, but they must not be presented as reviews of this product. That is why this post is written as a pre-purchase check, not a review.

![Transferring photos from a mini camera illustration](images/03-transfer.jpg)

This camera is not for people who want better photos than a smartphone. But for light snaps with a film-like texture, a desk object, and the surprise of handing it to someone, it works as $55 worth of fun. Before buying, lower your image-quality expectations and check whether a microSD card is needed, what the final total to Korea would be, and what warranty applies. If those terms are acceptable, the moment of picking up the film roll is worth the price.
