The mouse moved onto a finger — Prolo Ring, a trackpad on your index finger

You sit at a laptop, then read the news on a tablet, then hunt for the TV remote on the sofa. Every device switch means a different controller in your hand. The ring-shaped controller "Prolo Ring" from American startup Prolo wants to erase that switch entirely: one ring on your index finger controls your PC, tablet, phone, and TV. This is a write-up based on official materials and press coverage, not a hands-on review.

![A thumb sliding across the touch surface of a wide-band ring worn on the index finger — the touch area is 18×30 mm (illustration)](images/01-hero.jpg)

## What is this ring?

The product is called "Prolo Ring." An 18×30 mm capacitive touch surface sits on top of the ring, and your thumb moves the cursor, clicks, and scrolls on it. A touch strip on top called the "Modstrip" switches between four modes: Cursor, Navigation, Touch, and Air. It comes in four editions — Standard, Pro, Pro Bundle, and Special Edition — and the Standard edition is a plug-and-play type with 30+ built-in gestures ready to use.

## Why is it fun?

**First, gestures solve the limits of a finger-sized trackpad.** Moving a cursor across a whole screen on an 18×30 mm surface is hard. "Joystick Assist" keeps the cursor moving: swipe quickly from one edge of the trackpad to the other and hold, and the cursor keeps going. The farther your thumb sits from the center, the faster it moves; releasing stops it. Fine positioning still works with ordinary trackpad movements.

**Second, device switching ends on the ring.** Pair several devices over Bluetooth, then tap and hold on the Modstrip to drop the current connection and move to the next device. It uses standard Bluetooth HID, so core controls work without installing a driver or a dedicated app. Supported platforms include Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, plus compatible smart TVs and XR devices. One caveat: it connects to only one device at a time, so as Yanko Design notes, calling the switch "instant" is a bit of a stretch.

**Third, the Pro edition programs your finger.** More than 40 gestures can be assigned shortcuts, macros, and profiles, and air gestures let you control devices by moving your hand in the air. Push-to-talk can trigger AI dictation, but the ring has no microphone — the connected device handles voice input. Configuration is done in the free desktop app Prolo Studio.

![A hand with the ring switching control between a laptop, a tablet, and a wall-mounted TV at a desk — no need to pick up a different controller per device (illustration)](images/02-devices.jpg)

## The downsides

The ring form brings clear constraints. The 23 mm wide band does not match regular ring sizing, so you must test-wear and confirm your size from US 7–18 using the sizing kit or a 3D-printed sizer before fulfillment. Adjustment shims are included for fine tuning. It is not waterproof, so it has to come off before showering or swimming. Some interactions need both hands, and the small touch surface takes a learning period. Battery life is up to 10 hours of typical use, so all-day use needs an evening charge. Charging finishes within an hour on the magnetic cable.

## The editor's call

This ring is less a "mouse replacement" than a product for "the moments a mouse cannot reach." Its core appeal is not having to reach out: advancing slides during a presentation, controlling the TV from the sofa, or reading articles on a tablet. For precise graphic work or long writing sessions, a regular mouse and keyboard are still better. At $149 it is pricey for pure curiosity, but worth calculating if your work spans many devices. Just factor in the hassle of size confirmation before buying.
